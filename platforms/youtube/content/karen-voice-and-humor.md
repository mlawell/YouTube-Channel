# Karen's voice and humor

**Approved by Karen. Every line here is a suggestion she can strike.**

This is a scripting document, not a performance note, and that distinction is the
whole reason it exists.

Karen's videos are produced with **HeyGen for the avatar and ElevenLabs for the
voice.** A presenter standing in a kitchen can notice something funny and say it.
An avatar reads what it is given. So **every joke, every aside, every bit of
warmth has to be written into the script in advance, or it will not exist.**
Humor here is a deliverable with a word count, not a thing that happens on the
day.

That matters more than it sounds. `win-with-youtube-challenge.md` records that
YouTube runs **built-in AI language and imagery detection and throttles content
that indexes too high** (`D1-QA` 00:03:34, `D1-GS` 00:13:44), and names the tells
by name (`D1-QA` 00:05:11). The **"AI 20" rule** allows AI to do 80% of the work
and requires human intelligence to do the other 20% (`D1-QA` 00:04:07).

**On a channel that is genuinely machine-narrated, specific local human humor is
the 20%.** It is not decoration. It is the thing standing between this channel
and the throttle.

---

## The voice, in a paragraph

Karen is a **resident who happens to be the realtor**, not a realtor who has
researched a community. She and her husband Mike live in Phase 8. She is warm,
dry and unhurried, she tells you the bad part before you ask, and she is funny
the way a good neighbour is funny — at her own expense, never at yours. She
knows the street names are absurd and she loves them anyway. She has opinions and
gives them, but she never sells you the phase she thinks you should want. Rachel
Dau's wardrobe rule is the right calibration for the tone as well as the clothes:
speak as you would *"to eat lunch or have brunch with a 60-plus-year-old woman
and her husband"* (`D5-FS` 00:47:02).

**These buyers chose a Jimmy Buffett community on purpose.** They are in on the
joke. They are never the butt of it.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Dry, warm, peer-to-peer | Zany, snarky, or youth-coded |
| Self-deprecating — Karen is the punchline | Anyone else is the punchline |
| Specific to *this* community | Generic "Florida retirement" humor |
| Tell the bad part unprompted | Sell the phase |
| "I live here" as a fact, said once | "I live here" as a slogan, said eight times |
| Let a joke be small and move on | Land on it, pause for it, repeat it |
| Age as shared ground | Age as the joke |
| Silence where there's no joke | A forced bit because the slot was empty |

## Banned phrases and constructions

These are the tells. Some are named in the training; the rest are the standard
machine register.

### Named in the training (`D1-QA` 00:05:11)

- *"five things no other agent is telling you"* — and the whole "N things they don't want you to know" family
- *"this city's a vibe"* — and **"vibe"** as a noun generally
- **em-dashes**

### ⛔ The em-dash rule

**No em-dashes in any spoken line.** Two independent reasons:

1. It is a **named AI detection tell**. It is the single most recognisable one in written copy.
2. **ElevenLabs reads the cadence.** An em-dash produces a particular clipped hesitation that stacks up across a twenty-minute read and starts to sound synthetic, which on this channel is the exact impression we cannot afford.

Use a **comma**, a **full stop**, or the word **"and"**. A full stop is almost
always better than the dash it replaces, because Karen talks in short sentences.

> ⚠️ **Known debt.** The existing scripts were written before this rule and are
> full of em-dashes in spoken copy. They have deliberately **not** been rewritten,
> because bulk-editing spoken copy risks the facts. Instead every script now
> carries a production-checklist line: read the spoken lines aloud and replace
> every em-dash before the script goes to ElevenLabs. Prose, direction and tables
> are unaffected. This applies to **spoken lines only**.

### The rest of the register

Never write, in spoken copy:

*delve · tapestry · testament to · nestled · boasts · a hidden gem · look no
further · in today's fast-paced world · it's important to note · when it comes to
· the perfect blend of · elevate · unlock · seamless · curated · bustling ·
picturesque · breathtaking · stunning (of a house) · dive in · let's face it ·
at the end of the day · that being said · rest assured · a myriad of ·
whether you're X or Y, there's something for everyone*

Two structural bans:

- **The rule of three.** *"the beaches, the sunsets, and the lifestyle."* Machines love a tricolon. Use two things, or four.
- **The rhetorical-question opener.** *"Ever wondered what it's like to live in paradise?"* Karen opens with a fact or a contradiction, never a question she plans to answer herself.

## Signature details

The doctrine: build **things you always comment on**, so returning viewers get an
inside joke. Jesse's is single-bowl offset-drain sinks; Rachel's is big drawers
(`D1-QA` 00:47:38).

**Karen has two: the Buffett street names, and Mike.**

### 1. The street names

Full verified table:
[`street-names-buffett.md`](../../../properties/latitude-margaritaville-watersound/street-names-buffett.md).
**29 of 60 streets are confirmed** references to a real song, lyric, brand or
biographical fact. 28 fit the theme but trace to nothing. 3 are just roads.

**Only the confirmed 29 may be stated as fact on camera.** Parrotheads know this
catalogue better than we do, and a correction in the comments costs more than the
joke earned.

⚠️ **Three traps.** Do not attribute these to Buffett:

- **No Shoes Ct** is **Kenny Chesney** (2002). Not Buffett. This is the most correctable error available.
- **Cool Water Way** — no confirmed Buffett recording.
- **Escape Ave** — there is no Buffett song called "Escape."

⚠️ **The density trend is false.** The appealing story is that Buffett density
climbs by phase. It was counted: 1, 3, 7, 4, 2, 4, 3, 0, 4, 5. Correlation with
phase number is **r = +0.064**. Phase 3 is the densest, not Phase 10. **Do not
say it climbs.**

**What is true, and funnier:** Phase 8 — Karen's own — is the **only phase in the
community with zero confirmed references.** Out of sixteen plats she bought on
the one street set with no Jimmy Buffett in it.

### 2. Mike

Mike Lawell is Karen's husband. They live in Phase 8 together. He has volunteered
himself as material.

The model is **Ken**, Rachel's videographer, deliberately built into a recurring
character who **never shows his face**; *"come on in, Ken"* became the channel's
signature transition (`D3-GS` 00:25:27–00:25:50), and Ken tees up CTAs by asking
the question the viewer would ask (`D3-GS` 00:40:17).

**Mike is referenced, never interviewed. He is a character, not an authority.**

Why this earns its place:

- It is warm, self-deprecating, peer-to-peer humor, which is exactly right for a 55+ audience.
- **Most of these buyers are a couple deciding together.** A running Karen-and-Mike dynamic lets both of them see themselves in the video. That is a strategic advantage, not a decoration.
- It **reinforces residency without diluting authority.** Karen stays the expert. Mike is the neighbour. This does not violate the "never put other people on camera" rule (`D5-FS` 00:19:52), because he is never on camera and never the source of a fact.
- Competitor Mr. Beach **rents** this: his Latitude videos run through Rick, *"a local who actually lives in this 55+ community"* ([`mrbeach.md`](../../../knowledge/competitors/mrbeach.md)). Karen is Rick. Mike makes that unmissable.

**Keep it that way.** The moment Mike answers a question about plats or pricing,
the authority has transferred and the whole thing stops working.

## Density and placement

The framework's pacing point: humor is a **tool for moving fast through weak
visuals**. Rachel scouts for *"jokes and sweet spots"* rather than features, and
where there's little to look at she *"talks harder"* (`D5-FS` 00:24:18). A tour
is *"30 little micro videos"* — *"there's not much to see, so this needs to be
much to say."*

That is exactly this channel's problem. An avatar over a static map frame is the
weakest visual in the format, and it is most of the runtime.

**The rules:**

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **One beat every 90 to 120 seconds** | Roughly 10–14 in a 20-minute video, 5–7 in a deep dive |
| **Never two in a row** | Two consecutive beats reads as a comedy routine, and Karen is not doing a routine |
| **Never on top of a hard fact** | Not on a plat book and page, not on an address range, not on an acreage, not on an inventory number. **The map's authority is the product.** A joke sharing a breath with a plat citation devalues the citation |
| **Never attached to a number** | Say the number. Land it. Then be funny |
| **If a phase has no good joke, leave it alone** | A forced bit is worse than none. Phase 5B has two streets and nothing to say about them. Say nothing |
| **Densest where the visual is weakest** | Map-only stretches need the most; a photo or a cart run needs the least |
| **Never in the last 20 seconds** | That block is the redirect, and it carries one job |

**Placement inside a chapter.** Facts first, joke second, move on. The beat is
the exit from the chapter, not the entrance, so it never stands between the
viewer and the thing they scrubbed to find.

## How beats are marked in the scripts

```
`[HUMOR — optional, cut freely]`

> The spoken line.
```

`[HUMOR]` is Karen's to delete without consequence. Nothing structural depends on
one. `[MIKE]` marks a beat that needs **Mike's** agreement as well as Karen's.

Beats in the ten phase deep-dives are generated by
`tools/scripts/build_phase_scripts.py`, so a beat edited in the markdown will be
overwritten on the next data refresh. **Edit the generator**, or strike the line
at record time and leave the file alone.
