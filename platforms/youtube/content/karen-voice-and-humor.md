# Karen's voice and humor

**Approved by Karen. Every line here is a suggestion she can strike.**

This is a scripting document, not a performance note, and that distinction is the
whole reason it exists.

Karen's videos are produced with **HeyGen for the avatar and ElevenLabs for the
voice.** A presenter standing in a kitchen can notice something funny and say it.
An avatar reads what it is given. So **every joke, every aside, every bit of
warmth has to be written into the script in advance, or it will not exist.**
Humor here is a deliverable with a word count, not a thing that happens on the
day.

That matters more than it sounds. `win-with-youtube-challenge.md` records that
YouTube runs **built-in AI language and imagery detection and throttles content
that indexes too high** (`D1-QA` 00:03:34, `D1-GS` 00:13:44), and names the tells
by name (`D1-QA` 00:05:11). The **"AI 20" rule** allows AI to do 80% of the work
and requires human intelligence to do the other 20% (`D1-QA` 00:04:07).

**On a channel that is genuinely machine-narrated, specific local human humor is
the 20%.** It is not decoration. It is the thing standing between this channel
and the throttle.

## The voice, in a paragraph

Karen is a **resident who happens to be the realtor**, not a realtor who has
researched a community. She and her husband Mike live in Phase 8, on **Cool Water
Way**, with three cats. She is warm, dry and unhurried, she tells you the bad
part before you ask, and she is funny the way a good neighbour is funny — at her
own expense, never at yours. She knows the street names are absurd and she loves
them anyway. She has opinions and gives them, but she never sells you the phase
she thinks you should want. Rachel Dau's wardrobe rule is the right calibration
for the tone as well as the clothes: speak as you would *"to eat lunch or have
brunch with a 60-plus-year-old woman and her husband"* (`D5-FS` 00:47:02).

**These buyers chose a Jimmy Buffett community on purpose.** They are in on the
joke. They are never the butt of it.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Dry, warm, peer-to-peer | Zany, snarky, or youth-coded |
| Self-deprecating — Karen is the punchline | Anyone else is the punchline |
| **The joke is on the place, the paperwork, or us** | **The joke is on the theme, or on residents** |
| Specific to *this* community | Generic "Florida retirement" humor |
| Tell the bad part unprompted | Sell the phase |
| "I live here" as a fact, said once | "I live here" as a slogan, said eight times |
| Let a joke be small and move on | Land on it, pause for it, repeat it |
| Age as shared ground | Age as the joke |
| Silence where there's no joke | A forced bit because the slot was empty |

## Banned phrases and constructions

These are the tells. Some are named in the training; the rest are the standard
machine register.

### Named in the training (`D1-QA` 00:05:11)

- *"five things no other agent is telling you"* — and the whole "N things they don't want you to know" family
- *"this city's a vibe"* — and **"vibe"** as a noun generally
- **em-dashes**

### ⛔ The em-dash rule

**No em-dashes in any spoken line.** Two independent reasons:

1. It is a **named AI detection tell**. It is the single most recognisable one in written copy.
2. **ElevenLabs reads the cadence.** An em-dash produces a particular clipped hesitation that stacks up across a twenty-minute read and starts to sound synthetic, which on this channel is the exact impression we cannot afford.

Use a **comma**, a **full stop**, or the word **"and"**. A full stop is almost
always better than the dash it replaces, because Karen talks in short sentences.

> ⚠️ **Known debt.** The existing scripts were written before this rule and are
> full of em-dashes in spoken copy. They have deliberately **not** been rewritten,
> because bulk-editing spoken copy risks the facts. Instead every script now
> carries a production-checklist line: read the spoken lines aloud and replace
> every em-dash before the script goes to ElevenLabs. Prose, direction and tables
> are unaffected. This applies to **spoken lines only**.

### The rest of the register

Never write, in spoken copy:

*delve · tapestry · testament to · nestled · boasts · a hidden gem · look no
further · in today's fast-paced world · it's important to note · when it comes to
· the perfect blend of · elevate · unlock · seamless · curated · bustling ·
picturesque · breathtaking · stunning (of a house) · dive in · let's face it ·
at the end of the day · that being said · rest assured · a myriad of ·
whether you're X or Y, there's something for everyone*

Two structural bans:

- **The rule of three.** *"the beaches, the sunsets, and the lifestyle."* Machines love a tricolon. Use two things, or four.
- **The rhetorical-question opener.** *"Ever wondered what it's like to live in paradise?"* Karen opens with a fact or a contradiction, never a question she plans to answer herself.

## Signature details

The doctrine: build **things you always comment on**, so returning viewers get an
inside joke. Jesse's is single-bowl offset-drain sinks; Rachel's is big drawers
(`D1-QA` 00:47:38).

**Karen has two: the Buffett street names, and Mike.**

### 1. The street names

Full verified table:
[`street-names-buffett.md`](../../../properties/latitude-margaritaville-watersound/street-names-buffett.md).
**29 of 60 streets are confirmed** references to a real song, lyric, brand or
biographical fact. 28 fit the theme but trace to nothing. 3 are just roads.

**Only the confirmed 29 may be stated as fact on camera.** Parrotheads know this
catalogue better than we do, and a correction in the comments costs more than the
joke earned.

⚠️ **Three traps.** Do not attribute these to Buffett:

- **No Shoes Ct** is **Kenny Chesney** (2002). Not Buffett. This is the most correctable error available.
- **Cool Water Way** is **Bob Nolan**, written 1936, first recorded by the **Sons of the Pioneers** in 1941. There is no confirmed Buffett recording. The 1936 date is checked and safe to say. If it ever comes into doubt, say *"a 1930s cowboy song"* instead. Never state a year nobody has checked.
- **Escape Ave** — there is no Buffett song called "Escape."

⚠️ **The density trend is false.** The appealing story is that Buffett density
climbs by phase. It was counted: 1, 3, 7, 4, 2, 4, 3, 0, 4, 5. Correlation with
phase number is **r = +0.064**. Phase 3 is the densest, not Phase 10. **Do not
say it climbs.**

**What is true, and funnier:** Phase 8 — Karen's own — is the **only phase in the
community with zero confirmed references.** Out of sixteen plats she bought on
the one street set with no Jimmy Buffett in it. **Karen and Mike live on Cool
Water Way**, which sounds like a Buffett song and is not one. That is beat 8
below, and it is the sharpest version of the signature.

### 2. Mike

Mike Lawell is Karen's husband. They live in Phase 8 together, on Cool Water Way.
He has volunteered himself as material, and on **2026-08-23** he reviewed every
line that references him. See [Mike's editorial rule](#-mikes-editorial-rule)
below and [the nine approved beats](#-the-nine-approved-beats): **that set is
closed.** Nothing new goes in without him.

The model is **Ken**, Rachel's videographer, deliberately built into a recurring
character who **never shows his face**; *"come on in, Ken"* became the channel's
signature transition (`D3-GS` 00:25:27–00:25:50), and Ken tees up CTAs by asking
the question the viewer would ask (`D3-GS` 00:40:17).

**Mike is referenced, never interviewed. He is a character, not an authority.**

Why this earns its place:

- It is warm, self-deprecating, peer-to-peer humor, which is exactly right for a 55+ audience.
- **Most of these buyers are a couple deciding together.** A running Karen-and-Mike dynamic lets both of them see themselves in the video. That is a strategic advantage, not a decoration.
- It **reinforces residency without diluting authority.** Karen stays the expert. Mike is the neighbour. This does not violate the "never put other people on camera" rule (`D5-FS` 00:19:52), because he is never on camera and never the source of a fact.
- Competitor Mr. Beach **rents** this: his Latitude videos run through Rick, *"a local who actually lives in this 55+ community"* ([`mrbeach.md`](../../../knowledge/competitors/mrbeach.md)). Karen is Rick. Mike makes that unmissable.

**Keep it that way.** The moment Mike answers a question about plats or pricing,
the authority has transferred and the whole thing stops working.

## ⭐ Mike's editorial rule

On **2026-08-23** Mike sat down and reviewed every beat that referenced him, line
by line. Most of them were cut, and several were cut because they were **wrong
about the community**, not because they weren't funny. The full cut list and his
reasons are recorded below, because the reasons are the useful part.

What the survivors have in common is the most useful scripting rule this document
contains, because it predicts the next beat better than any style note:

> ### The joke is on the place, on paperwork, or on ourselves.
> ### Never on the theme, and never on residents.

Two things follow from it:

- **Mike as a generic sitcom husband was rejected every time.** *"He said he'd use the gym, he uses the pool"* works for any husband anywhere, which is exactly why it isn't funny. A beat that would survive a find-and-replace of the community name is not a beat.
- **Mocking the theme mocks the people who chose to live inside it.** Mike does not think the street names are ridiculous, and he would not let Karen say he does. Every viewer of this channel chose a Jimmy Buffett community on purpose. The absurdity is a shared pleasure, not a target.

### The two registers

They are different voices doing different jobs, and **they never compete.**

| Voice | Form | Approved examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Karen's own reversals** | First person, warm, **she** is the one caught out | bingo, Phase 8's streets, the cats |
| **Mike** | Third person, deadpan, short flat sentences | bring a cart, the Hawaiian shirt |

She is generous about herself and dry about Mike. That reads as **affection
rather than complaint**, which is what keeps it safe for an audience that is
mostly couples watching together. Reverse it — Karen dry about herself, Mike the
warm one — and it curdles.

**Never put a Karen reversal and a Mike beat back to back.** One of them always
loses.

### The "I said I'd never ___" formula

Mike supplied this, and it is the channel's best comedic device. It has a name
now so it can be reused deliberately:

> ## "I said I'd never ___. Now it's one of my favorite things."

Why it works, in three parts:

1. **The 55+ audience has all lived it.** Resisting a thing coded as "retiree", then loving it, is the shared experience of the entire demographic. Recognition, not observation.
2. **Self-deprecating without bitterness.** Karen loses the argument with herself, so nobody else has to lose one.
3. **It makes humour carry information.** Each instance names a **real amenity**, which is the direct fix for Rachel Dau's low-visual problem: *"there's not much to see, so this needs to be much to say"* (`D5-FS` 00:24:18). A joke that also tells you bingo happens on Tuesdays is doing two jobs in one breath.

**Approved instances: two.** Bingo (Karen) and the Hawaiian shirt (Mike).

**Explicitly rejected by Mike:** pickleball, golf cart to dinner, and *"I said
I'd never learn the words."*

⛔ **Do not generate more.** The formula is recorded so the approved ones can be
read correctly, not so the set can be grown. A new instance needs Mike.

## Reactions Karen records herself

Beat 9 carries a `[slight giggle]`. That cue is a production instruction, not
punctuation, and it needs its own rule.

**A synthesised laugh is one of the clearest AI tells there is.** ElevenLabs will
attempt it and it will sound like an attempt. The framework already warns that
YouTube runs built-in AI detection and **throttles content that indexes too
high** (`D1-QA` 00:03:34). A fake laugh sitting on top of the funniest line in
the video is the worst possible place to spend that budget.

### ⛔ `[...]` cues are edit instructions. They are never ElevenLabs input.

**This is the load-bearing line in this section.** Every square-bracket cue in
this repo — `[slight giggle]`, `[KAREN]`, `[CART]`, `[INVENTORY]`, `[FRAME]`,
`[BEAT]` — is direction for a human. **Strip all of them before the script goes
to the API**, exactly as the em-dash rule already requires.

`[slight giggle]` **is not a usable fallback**, and it fails two different ways
depending on which model is behind the voice. Both were checked against the
ElevenLabs documentation on 2026-08-23:

| Model | What happens to `[slight giggle]` |
| --- | --- |
| **v2 family** (Multilingual v2, Flash v2.5, the usual production voices) | ⚠️ **The words are spoken aloud.** ElevenLabs' own best-practices page: the model *"will still speak out the emotional delivery guides."* Karen literally says "slight giggle" mid-sentence |
| **Eleven v3** | Audio tags work, but the vocabulary is **fixed** and `[slight giggle]` is not in it. An unsupported tag is not a giggle |

The v2 failure is the dangerous one because **it is silent**. Nothing errors,
nothing warns, and it only surfaces when somebody listens to the whole take.
ElevenLabs' own mitigation is post-production: *"Remember to remove any emotional
guidance text in post-production."* Removing it **before** the API call is
cheaper.

**The supported Eleven v3 vocabulary, in full:**

| Category | Tags |
| --- | --- |
| Human reactions | `[laughs]` · `[clears throat]` · `[sighs]` |
| Emotions | `[curious]` · `[crying]` · `[mischievously]` |
| Delivery | `[whispers]` · `[shouts]` |

[ElevenLabs — how audio tags work with Eleven v3](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/core-capabilities/text-to-speech/how-do-audio-tags-work-with-eleven-v3-alpha).

### The documented fallback, and it is explicitly second-best

If a synthetic reaction is ever genuinely unavoidable, there is exactly one path
that works, and it is worse than Karen's own voice:

1. **Model must be `eleven_v3`.** Audio tags do nothing on the v2 family except get read out.
2. **`[laughs]` is the nearest supported tag.** It is a laugh, not a slight giggle, so expect it to be bigger than the beat wants.
3. **Better for this particular beat: `[mischievously]` placed *before* the lyric.** It colours the delivery of "no shoes, no shirt, no problems" without inserting a laugh at all, which is closer to what the line needs than a full `[laughs]` dropped after it.

**Treat this as the emergency route.** It exists so that record day has an
answer, not because it is the plan.

### For a scripted pause, use `<break>`

Beat 9 also needs a **pause before "In a Margaritaville community."** Do not rely
on punctuation for that. `<break time="0.5s" />` is documented ElevenLabs syntax
and `<break time="1s"/>` is documented for HeyGen's own TTS, so the same
construction survives whichever engine speaks the line. Unlike `[slight giggle]`,
this one is real input and is meant to be left in.

### ⚠️ HeyGen cannot make the face agree with the laugh

Checked against the HeyGen developer docs on 2026-08-23, recorded in full in
[`HEYGEN.md`](../../../tools/avatar/HEYGEN.md).

Handing HeyGen a finished audio track **is** supported — that is exactly what
`make_presenter_heygen.py` already does. But:

- **There is no API parameter that makes an avatar laugh or smile on cue.** No emotion presets, no expression control. The only expression-adjacent parameter is `expressiveness` (`high`/`medium`/`low`), it exists on **Avatar IV photo avatars only**, and it is an **energy dial**, not an emotion selector.
- **Non-speech audio is undocumented.** The engine is speech-to-mouth: it re-animates the **mouth region** against the waveform. Cheeks, eyes and brow stay in the avatar's default neutral.

So a real laugh over the avatar gets you a **laughing voice on a neutral face**,
which is more jarring than no laugh at all. That is worse than the problem the
reaction library was solving.

> ### ⛔ The scripting rule this forces
> **Cut away from the avatar over every recorded reaction.** Put the giggle, the
> sigh or the breath over **B-roll, a map frame, or the street-sign photo**, and
> come back to the avatar on the next spoken sentence.

This is a **scripting** constraint, not an editing preference: the cutaway has to
be planned, and the shot has to exist. For beat 9 the cutaway is free — the
**No Shoes Ct sign** is already on the shot list and the beat is about that sign,
so the giggle plays over the exact image it refers to.

> ### ⭐ This generalises, and it is bigger than the giggle
> There is **no real footage of Karen and none is coming**, so every appearance
> is synthetic. A face that cannot change expression is wrong for comedy and
> perfectly fine for sincerity, which gives the governing rule:
>
> **Spend the synthetic face on sincerity. Spend the cutaways on comedy.**
>
> **Any** moment needing visible emotion cuts away, not just the giggle. Each of
> the nine beats has a designated cutaway image, and the face-time budget, the
> corner-inset format and the full asset inventory are in
> [`karen-presenter-treatment.md`](karen-presenter-treatment.md).

### ⭐ Strong recommendation: a reaction library

**Karen records her own reactions once, and they are dropped in on the edit.**

Record a small reusable set in one sitting, clean, no room noise, several takes
of each:

| Reaction | Where it earns its place |
| --- | --- |
| **Slight giggle** | Beat 9. The self-aware ones generally |
| **Fuller laugh** | Rare. Only where she genuinely finds it funny |
| **Sigh** | Ahead of the candour beats, before *"here's the one thing I'd tell you honestly"* |
| **"Mmm"** | Agreement with herself, ahead of a correction |
| **Short breath in** | Before a hard fact, so the fact has a beat of air in front of it |

Why this is worth the half hour:

- It is **the cheapest 20% of the "AI 20" rule** (`D1-QA` 00:04:07) available anywhere in the pipeline. A real human reaction over a synthetic read is the exact human intelligence the rule asks for.
- **Record once, use forever.** The same five files serve every video on the channel.
- It fixes the one thing an avatar cannot do: **react.** Karen's HeyGen avatar reads. **Those five files are the only moments in a twenty minute video where an actual human is present.** That is why they are worth planning a cutaway around rather than dropping on top of a neutral face.

**Rule:** no laugh, sigh or breath in any script is ever synthesised. If Karen
hasn't recorded it, the cue gets cut, not faked. And wherever one is used, **the
picture cuts away from the avatar for its duration.**

## Density and placement

The framework's pacing point: humor is a **tool for moving fast through weak
visuals**. Rachel scouts for *"jokes and sweet spots"* rather than features, and
where there's little to look at she *"talks harder"* (`D5-FS` 00:24:18). A tour
is *"30 little micro videos"* — *"there's not much to see, so this needs to be
much to say."*

That is exactly this channel's problem. An avatar over a static map frame is the
weakest visual in the format, and it is most of the runtime.

**The rules:**

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **One beat every 90 to 120 seconds** | Roughly 10–14 in a 20-minute video, 5–7 in a deep dive |
| **Never two in a row** | Two consecutive beats reads as a comedy routine, and Karen is not doing a routine |
| **Never on top of a hard fact** | Not on a plat book and page, not on an address range, not on an acreage, not on an inventory number. **The map's authority is the product.** A joke sharing a breath with a plat citation devalues the citation |
| **Never attached to a number** | Say the number. Land it. Then be funny |
| **If a phase has no good joke, leave it alone** | A forced bit is worse than none. Phase 5B has two streets and nothing to say about them. Say nothing |
| **Densest where the visual is weakest** | Map-only stretches need the most; a photo or a cart run needs the least |
| **Never in the last 20 seconds** | That block is the redirect, and it carries one job |

**Placement inside a chapter.** Facts first, joke second, move on. The beat is
the exit from the chapter, not the entrance, so it never stands between the
viewer and the thing they scrubbed to find.

## How beats are marked in the scripts

```
`[HUMOR — optional, cut freely]`

> The spoken line.
```

`[HUMOR]` is Karen's to delete without consequence. Nothing structural depends on
one. `[MIKE]` marks a beat in Mike's register. **Every `[MIKE]` beat in the
scripts is already approved by him** as of 2026-08-23; the marker now means *read
it flat and verbatim*, not *go and ask him*. A **new** one still needs him.

Beats in the ten phase deep-dives are generated by
`tools/scripts/build_phase_scripts.py`, so a beat edited in the markdown will be
overwritten on the next data refresh. **Edit the generator**, or strike the line
at record time and leave the file alone. The approved set lives in the `HUMOR`
and `MIKE` dicts near the top of that file, and regeneration is deterministic:
re-running the script twice on the same data produces byte-identical output.
