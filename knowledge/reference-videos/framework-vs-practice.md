# Framework vs. Practice

What the Channel Junkies material *teaches*, what the best relocation channels
*actually do*, and where those two things come apart.

This is the argumentative document in [`knowledge/reference-videos/`](README.md).
It is built from 18 measured videos and 330 videos' worth of channel-level
runtime data — see [`catalog.md`](catalog.md) and [`channels.md`](channels.md).

**A catalog that simply agreed with the playbook would be worthless.** The point
of measuring real videos is to find out which parts of the doctrine survive
contact with evidence. Most of it does. Three things do not.

> **The honesty clause.** Every one of these videos is a lead-generation video,
> and **we cannot see anyone's leads.** Views are a bad proxy for a business
> outcome and are treated as one throughout. Where the evidence contradicts the
> framework on *views*, that is not proof the framework is wrong about
> *conversion* — it just means the framework's claim is untested here, and it is
> written that way. The sample is also deliberately selected for high performers,
> so nothing below is a controlled comparison.

---

## The three findings

1. **[The price rule is about numbers, not bands.](#-finding-1-the-price-rule-is-about-numbers-not-bands)**
   "Never reveal the price until the end" is a rule about a *specific home's
   number*. A price *range* is an orientation device and appears in the first
   twenty seconds of the best community videos in this set — including in a home
   tour that also holds its number to 99%.
2. **[18–28 minutes is the floor, not the target.](#-finding-2-1828-minutes-is-the-floor-not-the-target)**
   Every real-estate channel measured has a median runtime at or above 18:49.
   The founders' own channels sit at 25:41 and 35:48.
3. **[The taught closing order is not the practised closing order.](#-finding-3-the-taught-closing-order-is-not-the-practised-closing-order)**
   CTA → price → redirect is the doctrine. Redirect → price is what the
   reference channel actually publishes, on both of its home tours, including
   the one used to teach the lesson.

## ⭐ Finding 1: the price rule is about numbers, not bands

**What the framework teaches.** Characteristic 7: *"The reveal is the price, and
it belongs at the very end."* Order it CTA → price → redirect, because *"we
always see the retention just immediately dump as soon as you give the price"*
(`D3-GS` 00:44:53). The framework grants exactly one exception, under the hook:
price may appear early *when affordability is the hook*, and *"even then only as
a contrast, never a number"* (`D3-GS` 00:22:42).

**What the evidence shows.** Sorting all 13 measurable videos by where the first
real-estate price lands produces no clean split by channel — but a very clean one
by **what kind of price it is**:

| Kind of price | n | Earliest | Median | Latest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Band** (a range: *"the 350s"*, *"400,000 up to 1.5 million"*) | 9 | **0.7%** | 14.1% | 70.4% |
| **Number** (a figure attached to one specific home) | 4 | 4.2% | **85.6%** | 86.7% |

And the three videos that have a genuine closing **reveal** — all of them single
properties — reveal at **99.4%, 99.0% and 91.0%** of runtime.

**The reconciliation.** Rachel Dau's *Shockingly Affordable* tour is the whole
argument in one video. It states a price **band at 0:12** — *"with views like
this for under a million dollars"* — and holds the **number to 28:24 of 28:41**,
99% through. She is not being inconsistent. She is doing both things at once,
and the framework's own affordability exception describes it precisely.

So the rule as practised is:

> **The band orients. The number pays off.**
> A range tells the viewer whether to keep watching and can be given
> immediately. A specific home's price is the payoff and belongs at the end.
> **Community videos have no specific home — so they have no reveal, and the
> band is all they have.** That is why they appear to "give the price early."
> They are not breaking the rule; the rule has nothing to hold back.

**Where this does *not* hold — stated plainly.** Bands do not always come early.
Three videos in this set put their first band well past halfway:

| Video | Band at | Why, as far as we can tell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jelinek — *Cocoa Beach, 40 years* | 59.2% | Cost is a chapter inside a place-portrait, not the premise |
| Cunha — *Cape Coral's Biggest Dealbreaker* | 63.2% | Spends the first half on features, then prices the trade-off |
| Mr. Beach — *Full Tour of Margaritaville* | 70.4% | An amenity-and-lifestyle tour; the model prices arrive as an aside |

So "bands early" is a **tendency, not a law** — and the tendency tracks how
central budget is to the video's promise. Where affordability is in the title
(*Affordable 55+ Communities*, *Shockingly Affordable*), the band is inside 90
seconds. Where the promise is lifestyle, it drifts late.

**What Karen should take from this.** Her phases video is a community explainer
with no single property in it. It has **no reveal to hold**. Withholding the
price range would not build tension — it would fail to answer the viewer's first
question. The Latitude phases are *not priced differently from one another*,
which is itself the orienting fact, and it belongs early.

## ⭐ Finding 2: 18–28 minutes is the floor, not the target

**What the framework teaches.** 18–28 minutes, with average view duration ≥ 7
minutes absolute as Limiter #2.

**What the founders' own channels do.** Median runtime across every channel
measured, computed from 45–60 recent videos each:

| Channel | Median | ≥ 18 min | Median views | n |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Kyler Ferris — Houston** | **35:48** | **45 / 45** | 6,300 | 45 |
| **Mr. Beach — Emerald Coast** | 27:38 | 27 / 30 | 2,850 | 30 |
| **Wilkey & Dau — Portland** *(the original channel)* | 25:41 | 37 / 45 | 1,300 | 45 |
| Craig Cunha — Fort Myers | 23:04 | 34 / 45 | 1,800 | 45 |
| Rachel Dau — Idaho | 21:41 | 41 / 60 | 2,000 | 60 |
| David Jelinek — Melbourne | 18:49 | 32 / 60 | 579 | 60 |
| *Channel Junkies — coaching channel* | *12:38* | *15 / 45* | *241* | *45* |

Kyler Ferris has **not published a single video under 20 minutes** in his last
45. His top performers run 33:10, 32:37, 46:16 and 78:49. The original Portland
channel's best video is 45:04 and its second-best is **58:11**.

**The uncomfortable part.** The only channel in the set that makes short videos
is the **coaching channel that teaches the framework** — and it is by a wide
margin the worst-performing channel here: ~20,800 subscribers against a median
of **241 views per video**, roughly 1% of its own subscriber base. Its two
best-performing videos are its two longest (53:08 and 58:23).

That is not a gotcha. A coaching channel sells to a tiny professional audience
and is not trying to rank for relocation intent — different job, different
metrics. But it does mean **the 12:38 median is not a model to copy**, and it is
worth noticing that even there, longer won.

**The honest conclusion.** 18–28 minutes is where a channel becomes viable, not
where it should aim. Nothing in this data suggests an upper bound. What it
suggests is that **length must be earned by content** — Conroe gets 78 minutes
because there is 78 minutes of Conroe to cover.

**What Karen should take from this.** Her phases script targets **19–22
minutes**. That is below the median of *every real-estate channel in this set*
and roughly **half** what her direct competitor publishes on the same community
(Mr. Beach: 42:03 and 46:11 on Latitude Margaritaville Watersound). See
[the phases video note](../../platforms/youtube/content/latitude-phases-explained/README.md).

## ⭐ Finding 3: the taught closing order is not the practised closing order

**What the framework teaches.** Characteristic 8, and the scripted form is
explicit: CTA, *then* price, *then* redirect. Jesse plays a clip where the
redirect lands before the price and marks it **slightly wrong** — *"the editor
should have clipped that out"* (`D3-GS` 00:54:16).

**We found that clip.** It is Rachel Dau's
[*Tour this SHOCKINGLY AFFORDABLE Home in TOP Boise Master-Planned Community
[AVIMOR]*](https://youtu.be/kkCcBR87Hdc), and the captions close at 28:24 with
almost exactly the words quoted in the Day 3 session — including the figure
**$969,880**. Anyone learning this lesson can now watch the exact video, at the
exact timestamp, and see what "slightly wrong" looks like.

**But it is not an isolated slip.** Her *best-performing video of all* — the RV
garage tour, 24,810 views — closes the same way:

> *"If you want more videos like this, you're going to have to click on this
> link, and we'll meet you over there right now. $1,750,000 on this house."*

Redirect, then price. **Both** measured home tours on the reference channel do
the thing the doctrine says not to do. The hard lead-generation CTA is not in
that closing block at all — on the RV tour the last call/text ask lands around
23:43, roughly nine minutes before the reveal.

**How to read this.** Two possibilities, and the data cannot separate them:

1. The rule is right and the channel is imperfectly executing its own doctrine —
   which is exactly what Jesse says on air.
2. The rule matters less than taught, since the channel's biggest video breaks it.

What can be said without hedging: **the taught order and the published order
differ, on the reference channel, on its best work, repeatedly.** Anyone told
"this is how the top channel does it" should know that it mostly isn't.

The underlying mechanic is still sound and worth keeping: *end on the price, not
on an ask*, so the last thing the viewer hears is the answer they came for.

## The eight characteristics, one at a time

| # | Characteristic | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Call-out | ⚠️ **Contested** — half the top performers don't have one |
| 2 | Hook | ✅ **Holds**, and its price exception explains Finding 1 |
| 3 | Transition | — Not measurable from captions |
| 4 | Pacing | — Not measurable without retention graphs |
| 5 | Re-hooks | ✅ **Holds** where observable |
| 6 | Call to action | ✅ **Holds** — practice is even more patient than taught |
| 7 | The reveal | ⚠️ **Needs the band/number distinction** — see Finding 1 |
| 8 | Redirect | ❌ **Diverges** — see Finding 3 |

### 1 — The call-out ⚠️

**Taught.** Mandatory in *every* video, under 3–4 seconds, driver city then step
down to the sub-market. Its job: make the viewer *"look in the mirror and say,
that's me"*.

**Observed.** Only **6 of 16** measurable videos open with one. And in this
sample the videos *without* a call-out have the higher median view count
(≈10,200 vs ≈3,500).

Take that number with real caution — the sample is small, selected, and views
are not leads. But it cannot be waved away either, because the pattern includes
the two most relevant videos in the entire catalog:

- **Mr. Beach**, Karen's direct competitor, opens his 22,825-view Latitude
  Watersound tour with *"Hey guys, what's up? It's Mr. Beach hanging out with
  you today…"* — a channel-brand open, precisely what the framework says not to
  do. It is the highest-viewed video on Karen's own subject in this set.
- **The Villages** video (313,608 views) and **Explore55Plus** (58,697) have no
  call-out either.

**A likely explanation, offered as an argument rather than a measurement.** The
call-out's job is self-identification — *is this video for me?* On browse and
suggested traffic, **the title and thumbnail now do that job before the viewer
ever presses play.** "EVERY New Home Area in Viera Florida Right Now" and
"Affordable 55+ Communities in Central Florida" *are* call-outs; they just aren't
spoken. Jesse's rule was formulated when the opening seconds carried that load
alone.

**Recommendation: keep the call-out anyway.** It costs four seconds, it is free,
it demonstrably does no harm on the channels that use it, and it is the single
cheapest fix available. But the claim that omitting it kills a video is **not
supported by this data**, and Karen should not be told otherwise.

### 2 — The hook ✅

**Taught.** The number-one thing besides price, stacked, call-out plus hook under
30 seconds. Price may appear early *only* when affordability is the hook, and
then only as contrast.

**Observed.** Holds, and the exception is more load-bearing than the rule. Both
of the strongest openings in the catalog use a price band *as* the hook inside
20 seconds:

- Rachel, 0:12 — *"…with views like this for under a million dollars"*
- Jelinek, 0:17 — *"…ranging from 400,000 up to over 1.5 million. And that's a
  price range that almost anybody can afford."*

This is the framework describing its own escape hatch — and in practice the
escape hatch is the main road for community content.

### 3 — The transition — not measured

Getting off the driveway and through the front door is a **visual** edit.
Captions cannot see it, and no claim is made here.

### 4 — Pacing — not measured

Pacing is defined in the material as something you read off the **retention
graph**, which is private channel data. Runtime is the only visible proxy, and
it points one way: see Finding 2.

### 5 — Re-hooks ✅

**Taught.** Plant a question early, answer it late.

**Observed.** Where visible in captions, textbook. Jelinek's Viera map tour, at
0:33, thirty-three seconds in:

> *"And wait till the end because I'm going to reveal the biggest mistake new
> home buyers make when buying a new home in today's market. And this mistake
> can cost you a lot of time, money, and frustration."*

That is a re-hook doing exactly the taught job, in the same breath as the
call-out and the price band. Karen's phases video has an obvious equivalent
available and currently unused: *which phase would I not buy in, and why.*

### 6 — Call to action ✅

**Taught.** One at a time. Deposits before withdrawals. First spoken CTA around
four minutes; the hard ask at the end.

**Observed.** Holds at the median — and practice is *more* patient than doctrine.
Across the 14 videos where a first spoken CTA is detectable, the median lands at
**7:34**, comfortably later than the taught four minutes. Nobody in the
successful set opens with an ask.

But the spread is very wide — **0:04 to 20:01** — so this is a central tendency,
not a pattern. *These CTA timings are regex-derived and are offered as
indicative, not exact; unlike the price positions, they were not individually
hand-verified.*

The two extremes are instructive in opposite directions. The *Margaritaville
Daytona Beach* tour asks within the first ten seconds and is one of the shorter,
lower-view entries in its group. Cunha's *Dealbreaker* waits until 20:01 of
20:24 — essentially the whole video is deposit, and the withdrawal is the last
thing in it.

### 7 — The reveal ⚠️

See [Finding 1](#-finding-1-the-price-rule-is-about-numbers-not-bands). The rule
is sound but stated too broadly. As written — *"the price, and only at the very
end"* — it is a **home-tour** rule. Extended to community content it produces a
video that refuses to answer its own premise.

Amenity tours go further and skip price entirely: Jelinek's Del Webb amenity
centre runs 26:18 with **no price spoken at all** and outperforms his own priced
map tour four to one on views. "What it costs" and "what it's like" are allowed
to be different videos.

### 8 — The redirect ❌

See [Finding 3](#-finding-3-the-taught-closing-order-is-not-the-practised-closing-order).
The beat itself is common — **7 of 16** measurable videos redirect inside the
final 90 seconds, and every video in the catalog that has a price reveal also
has a redirect. It is the **ordering** that diverges.

## Two more conflicts worth recording

### The co-host problem

**Taught.** Day 5 is explicit: don't put other people on camera; *you* are the
character viewers subscribe to. The videographer may become a recurring
*off-camera* voice — the "come on in, Ken" pattern is endorsed by name.

**Observed.** Rachel follows this exactly: Ken is a voice, not a face, and the
banter is between presenter and camera.

**Mr. Beach does the opposite, and wins.** Both Latitude Watersound videos are
built around Rick, an on-camera co-host introduced as *"my good friend Rick.
Rick knows everything about Margaritaville."* Those two videos are 22,825 and
10,082 views — the strongest results on Karen's exact subject in this catalog.

The distinction the framework is really drawing is probably **authority
transfer**, not headcount: don't hand your credibility to a builder's rep who
will still be there when you aren't. Rick reads as a resident and a friend, not
a salesperson, and the format is closer to two neighbours walking around than to
an interview. Karen has an obvious equivalent — her husband, also a Phase 8
resident — and this evidence suggests it is worth trying, contrary to a literal
reading of the doctrine.

### The title problem

**Taught.** Days 1–2 warn against news and hype titles — they attract the wrong
traffic and kill channels.

**Observed.** Kyler Ferris's single best video in 45 is
[*Houston Housing Is Breaking ⚠️ (Here's What No One Wants to Admit)*](https://youtu.be/9WWYVahAhT0)
at **34,000 views** — a market-news headline with a warning emoji, published by
the channel behind the framework's own **4% conversion** benchmark
(`D3-VIP` 00:18:33).

**Both sides, honestly.** The framework's own caveat covers this: a news title
can win *views* while failing the *business*, because it pulls people who want
commentary rather than people who want to move. We can see his views. **We cannot
see his leads.** And the 4% figure attached to that channel is notably the *low*
end of the range Channel Junkies quotes, against Boise's 16% — which is at least
consistent with the warning being right about conversion even while the title
wins on reach. Not proof; a coherent reading.

## What this changes for Karen

| Finding | Action on the phases video |
| --- | --- |
| Bands orient, numbers pay off | Give the price range early. There is no single home to reveal, and "the phases are not priced differently" is the orienting fact viewers most need |
| 18–28 min is a floor | 19–22 minutes is below every real-estate channel median here, and roughly half Mr. Beach's runtime on the same community. Reconsider the target |
| Redirect vs. price ordering | Not applicable — no price reveal. But **end on the answer, not on an ask** |
| Call-out is contested but free | Keep it. Four seconds, no downside, and it is already a planned revision |
| Re-hooks are underused | Add one in the first 30 seconds: *which phase would I not buy in* |
| Chapters are rare but cluster in reference content | Keep them. Only 5 of 18 videos have chapters — and the two most chaptered are reference guides, exactly what this video is |
| Co-hosts are riskier in doctrine than in practice | Karen's husband is a Phase 8 resident. Worth a test, against the letter of the rule |
