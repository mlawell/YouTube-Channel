## ⭐ The Seven Limiter Framework (Day 2 — the core of the program)

A "waterfall": each limiter unlocks the next, and **every limiter carries a weighted
score** (`D2-GS` 00:06:26, 00:21:04). Full list as recapped in one breath at
`D2-QA` 00:17:22–00:17:55:

| # | Limiter | Target | Where to read it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Consistency** | ≥ **1 video/week** (2 is better) | Studio → Content (upload cadence) |
| 2 | **Average view duration** | ≥ **7 minutes** | Analytics → per-video + channel AVD |
| 3 | **TV / big-screen views** | ≥ **50%** (top channels hit 70%) | Analytics → Audience → device type |
| 4 | **Browse + Suggested traffic** | > **70%** — *the most heavily weighted limiter* | Analytics → Reach → Traffic sources |
| 5 | **No ads** | Zero paid traffic pointed at your videos | n/a (a decision, not a metric) |
| 6 | **No Shorts** | Zero Shorts on the channel | Studio → Content → filter Shorts |
| 7 | **Portfolio depth** | **15+ videos with 50+ views** in the past 7 days | Channel Grader |

> *"Every limiter has a weighted measurement. The number one measurement on any channel
> to get the best performance is having your browse and suggested traffic above 70."*
> — `D2-GS` 00:21:04

**Payoff when all seven are met:** *"more views from the right watcher… more watch time
by the highest intent viewers… more credibility… more leads and more profit"*
(`D2-GS` 00:34:00).

### 1 — Consistency
- One video a week is the floor; **two per week** is the working standard (`D2-GS` 00:08:02, `D1-GS` 00:56:20).
- **Never bulk-upload.** Film in bulk, release on schedule — *"YouTube is a pipeline game… if you overload it, all of those videos just step on each other"* (`D2-QA` 00:19:02).
- Keep **"two in the queue"** — two videos already uploaded and scheduled (`D2-GS` 00:10:58).
- Best days: **Wednesday + Saturday**. Saturday is when relocating families have time; Wednesday has almost no competition and feeds viewers into Saturday (`D2-GS` 00:11:11, `D1-GS` 00:57:45). Post times: Sat **9am local**, Wed **5pm local** (`D2-QA` 00:04:05).
- Put your **best** video on Wednesday — discovered by accident on Jesse's wife's channel (`D1-VIP` 00:44:49).
- Avoid snow/seasonal footage — YouTube treats it as time-sensitive and it fizzles (`D2-GS` 00:10:29).
- Skip the **Premiere** feature: *"I don't think it does anything"* (`D2-GS` 00:11:07, `D1-QA` 01:03:13).

### 2 — Average view duration ≥ 7 minutes
- The math: a good video is watched **~28%** through, so a **21–22 minute** video lands just under 7 minutes AVD (`D2-GS` 00:13:16).
- Lengths: **talking head / informational 15–20 min**; **home tours and vlogs 18–28 min** (`D2-GS` 00:13:49, `D1-GS` 00:56:25).
- Climb in **micro-steps** — if a 12-minute video yields 3–4 min AVD, go to 13, then 14 (`D2-GS` 00:16:51).
- Voiceover-over-B-roll rarely clears 10 min AVD; a live filmed walk-through does (`D1-QA` 00:48:32).
- Big drops in AVD/CTR when a video breaks out are normal — YouTube is testing it on a wider audience (`D1-VIP` 00:39:09).
- > *"It isn't that the consumer has a short attention span. It's just that we make a lot of bad videos."* — `D2-GS` 00:14:17

### 3 — TV views ≥ 50%
- YouTube is the #1 TV network — Neil Mohan at TV Connected Con: *"We're not trying to be TV. TV is trying to be us."* (`D2-GS` 00:17:44).
- TV viewers watch ~2× longer, subscribe more, and are the ones who call you: *"my family and I watch your guys' show every single Saturday morning"* (`D2-GS` 00:19:07).
- Mobile as your #1 device = a symptom of Shorts, ads, or short searchable content. Correct order is **TV → tablet/desktop → mobile** (`D2-GS` 00:17:13).
- TV % is an **output** of limiters 1–2, not a thing you act on directly.
- Watch your own videos back **on a TV** to see what viewers see (`D2-GS` 00:18:18).

### 4 — Browse + Suggested > 70% (highest weight)
- **Browse** = homepage distribution. **Suggested** = "up next". Together they are what Studio labels **YouTube Recommended** — that *is* the algorithm. **Search is not** (`D2-GS` 00:26:42).
- Search is only **10–15%** of YouTube traffic (`D1-GS` 00:39:23, `D1-QA` 00:41:25).
- > *"If you're focused on search traffic, you're literally fighting the algorithm."* — `D2-GS` 00:27:13
- Below 70% means **6–10× viewership growth is sitting on the table**; browse+suggested impressions should be **6–10× your search impressions** (`D2-GS` 00:27:47, `D1-VIP` 00:37:44).
- Jesse's guess: browse+suggested is the #1 limiter on **~90%** of channels in the room (`D2-GS` 00:20:48).
- **The trap:** you can score >70% browse and still get zero leads if that traffic is *local*. News-style titles pass the metric and fail the business (`D2-GS` 00:21:22).

### 5 & 6 — No ads, no Shorts
- Ads and Shorts do identical damage: tank AVD, collapse browse/suggested, flip your device mix to mobile. *"Ads and shorts are the two worst things you can do for your channel"* (`D1-VIP` 00:26:41).
- > *"No one is going to watch you for seven minutes and then watch your competitor for 30 seconds on your channel and then choose them over you. That is absurd."* — `D2-GS` 00:28:38
- > *"Imagine making the most beautiful recipe. And then at the very end, someone dumps like a gallon of ammonia in there. That's what putting shorts on your YouTube channel [is]."* — `D2-GS` 00:30:53
- **Delete existing Shorts — do not unlist.** *"150% yes… just delete and keep deleting until there's no more Shorts left"* (`D1-QA` 00:07:30; `D2-GS` 00:30:44; `D1-VIP` 01:00:49).
- **Ad revenue ≠ running ads.** Leave monetization ON. Jesse asked YouTube directly: monetized vs not makes **0% difference** to distribution or leads (`D1-QA` 00:52:53, `D2-GS` 00:28:09).
- Don't clip long-form into Reels/Shorts to "feed" the channel either — check how many Shorts viewers ever returned: *"I will guarantee you it is probably zero"* (`D2-QA` 00:13:48).

### 7 — 15+ videos with 50+ views in the past 7 days
- Proof YouTube is pushing your **whole portfolio**, not just the newest upload (`D2-GS` 00:31:27).
- Real numbers: Jesse's wife's channel **36**; the Houston channel up to **60**; the floor case — a client with **1,000 subs and ~200 views/video** still hits **15** (`D2-GS` 00:32:51–00:33:33).
- Run the Grader **Sat–Mon**; weekend viewing settles the number (`D2-GS` 00:31:36).

## ⭐ The eight characteristics of a high-impact video (Day 3 — the answer to "what makes a tour rank")

> *"So today I'm going to walk you through the eight characteristics that makes a video
> top notch."* — `D3-GS` 00:06:20

**How this list was actually delivered — read this before trusting the numbering.** Jesse
promises eight characteristics at `D3-GS` 00:06:20, then teaches them *narratively* by
playing one of his wife's home tours and pausing to comment. During that hour he numbers
exactly one of them out loud — *"a characteristic number four of a good video… I talk
about pacing"* (`D3-GS` 00:32:52). **The clean enumerated list only appears the next day**,
when he recaps it from memory at `D4-GS` 00:44:29–00:45:03 — and he has to stop and count,
realising he has left one out:

> *"Remember, it's following that sequence, the eight characteristics. It's the call-out,
> 'so you're thinking about moving to…'. It's the hook… so you got your hook, you got your
> re-hooks, you got your transitions, you got your pacing on point, you got your big
> reveal, and then you have your redirect — and I'm missing one, what is it? Oh, oh gosh
> — you guys, call to action. I was gonna say, I missed the biggest one."*
> — `D4-GS` 00:44:29–00:45:03 *(Whisper stutter artifacts removed)*

So the canonical eight, as Jesse himself counts them:

| # | Characteristic | In one line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Call-out** | *"So you're thinking about moving to [Driver City]…"* — under 3–4 seconds |
| 2 | **Hook** | The #1 thing about this property *besides price*, aimed at your avatar |
| 3 | **Transition** | Get off the driveway and through the front door — don't fill dead air |
| 4 | **Pacing** | Explicitly numbered #4 on air; read it off the retention graph |
| 5 | **Re-hooks** | Tease now, pay off later — keep unresolved questions open |
| 6 | **Call to action** | One at a time, undercover, *after* you've made deposits |
| 7 | **The reveal** | The price — and *only* at the very end |
| 8 | **Redirect** | Immediately send them to the next video/playlist |

> ⚠️ **Caveat, stated plainly:** the Day 3 walkthrough also teaches a **payoff** beat (see
> below) as a distinct, named part of the structure — *"it's the call-out, the hook,
> transition to the house, and then give them a really big payoff"* (`D3-GS` 00:34:02).
> The payoff does **not** appear in the Day 4 recap of eight. So the material really
> describes **nine** named beats and counts eight. Nothing here is invented to force the
> number; treat "payoff" as a ninth beat that Jesse teaches but does not count.

### 1 — The call-out
- Must be in **every single video**. Its job is to make the viewer *"look in the mirror and say, that's me"* (`D3-GS` 00:13:15–00:13:29).
- **Under 3 seconds, 4 seconds maximum** (`D3-GS` 00:19:52).
- Go one notch more specific than the obvious: *"you're thinking about relocating"* is too broad; *"you're thinking about relocating to [your driver city]"* is the line (`D3-GS` 00:13:55–00:14:21).
- **Always call out to the driver city, then step down** to the sub-market in the same breath: *"So you're thinking about moving here to Phoenix, Arizona. Well, today I'm in Queen Creek."* (`D3-GS` 00:19:27–00:19:39).
- Naming a suburb-only channel ("Living in South Denver") is a trap — a relocator doesn't know your compass points and assumes you don't cover the rest (`D3-GS` 00:02:49–00:03:21).

### 2 — The hook
- The hook is **whatever you think is the number-one thing about the video *besides price***, spoken directly to your avatar (`D3-GS` 00:19:57–00:20:12).
- Stack several. Jesse counted **~8 hooks in the first 20 seconds** of the example video (`D3-GS` 00:21:17–00:21:29).
- **Call-out + hook together in under 30 seconds, max** (`D3-GS` 00:22:26–00:22:37). In the worked example: **~4 seconds of call-out + ~20 seconds of hook, under 24 seconds total** (`D3-GS` 00:28:18–00:28:29).
- Hooks must **flow into each other**, not jump. Bad hooks are *"shifting pattern interrupts that are just like all over the place that don't flow nicely together"* (`D3-GS` 00:21:33–00:22:20).
- Build the hook out of your avatar's actual criteria. Jesse's Boise avatar is three things — **single-level home, quarter-acre lot or larger, RV bay** — and 70% of content is aimed at it (`D3-GS` 00:20:16–00:20:34).
- **The one time price may appear early** is when affordability *is* the hook, and even then only as a contrast, never a number: *"most of the clients we've helped relocate here have all bought million dollar plus homes. But today I'm going to be showing you guys something very affordable."* (`D3-GS` 00:22:42–00:23:31). This keeps both price bands watching.

### 3 — The transition
- The classic mistake: finishing the hook at the end of the driveway and then **filling the walk-up with filler** (`D3-GS` 00:24:31–00:24:53).
- The fix: **cut the moment the hook lands, and restart at the front door.** Selfie style — open the door and say *"all right, come on in guys."* With an operator — they stand inside, open the door, and you say *"come on in, Ken"* (`D3-GS` 00:24:53–00:25:25).
- Give the operator a name and let them become a recurring character: Ken never shows his face except in the occasional mirror, and *"come on in Ken"* is now the channel's signature (`D3-GS` 00:25:27–00:25:50).
- Going straight in is the default. **Linger outside only when the exterior earns it** (`D3-GS` 00:30:02–00:30:07). If your market's exteriors are repetitive — *"every house is just like brown with tile roof"* — **start in the backyard instead** (`D3-GS` 00:30:14–00:30:36).

### 4 — Pacing *(the one he numbers on air)*
- Call out features **without going deep on every one**: *"see how she's like nailing the features without going super deep on them, but it's still calling them out as she's going"* (`D3-GS` 00:32:33–00:32:46).
- **Read pacing off the retention graph.** You want the line *"as smooth as possible"* — *"if it's wildly going up and down, it just says your pacing, your cadence, your hooks, your re-hooks are all out of whack"* (`D3-GS` 00:53:00–00:53:13).
- Physically move through a room in a **zigzag, side to side**, so the camera operator can stand still and stay in front of you instead of constantly changing angle — Jesse compares it to how a police dog is trained to search (`D3-GS` 00:38:06–00:38:46).
- Energy is part of pacing. When you flag between films, **do jumping jacks** — *"we'd be on the streets doing jumping jacks… get the energy up, get your blood flow going"* (`D4-GS` 00:11:09–00:11:39).

### 5 — Re-hooks
- Plant a question early, answer it late. In the example, bedroom size and RV-bay depth are teased during the tour and only revealed in the final minute — **14×14 bedrooms** and a **44-foot-deep RV bay revealed with ~45 seconds left** (`D3-GS` 00:46:17–00:48:19).
- **Don't dump the specs up front.** Square footage isn't given until the closing segment: *"that's like another big thing that agents do, is they just spill out all of the details of the house in the first five seconds… if you're not interested in that size of house you're just not even going to watch it"* (`D3-GS` 00:47:02–00:47:21).
- Tease the drone shot before you cut to it — *"we'll give you some drone footage and show you what's going on over there in just a minute"* keeps them watching for it (`D3-GS` 00:31:57–00:32:10).

### 6 — Call to action (the "undercover" CTA)
- **One CTA at a time.** *"If you tell them to do three things… they're gonna do zero"* (`D3-GS` 00:36:16–00:36:29).
- **Deposits before withdrawals:** *"we have to make deposits into the bank before we can ask for a withdrawal from the ATM machine"* (`D3-GS` 00:36:29–00:36:41).
- In the worked example the **first spoken CTA lands ~4 minutes into the finished video — roughly 15–20 minutes into the actual filming** (`D3-GS` 00:40:37–00:40:52). The hard CTA is reserved for the very end (`D3-GS` 00:36:41–00:36:52).
- **On-screen CTAs carry the earlier load.** Name + branding appears in the first second, bottom-left; a subtle subscribe prompt around 1:39; a visual "thinking about moving to the area? call or text" card at 1:53 — *"it doesn't go ding ding or anything like that, it's just a subtle call-out"* (`D3-GS` 00:28:33–00:29:19, 00:37:24–00:37:41).
- If your first spoken CTA is 3–4 minutes in, **make sure the on-screen version is up sooner** (`D4-GS` 00:45:37–00:45:51).
- **Break the CTA immediately.** After asking, snap attention straight back to the house — *"Ken, check out these lights"* — because *"not everyone's going to be cool with the call to action"* (`D3-GS` 00:41:26–00:41:50).
- Let the videographer tee it up for you: Ken asks *"would this be considered a phantom home?"* so Rachel can answer with the offer (`D3-GS` 00:40:17–00:40:37).

### 7 — The reveal
- **The reveal is the price, and it belongs at the very end.** *"Everyone wants to know the price of the house. We watched the video for four minutes and you never even heard her say one thing about pricing"* (`D3-GS` 00:42:44–00:42:56).
- Order it **CTA → price → redirect**, never price → CTA: *"we always see the retention just immediately dump as soon as you give the price"* (`D3-GS` 00:44:53–00:45:14).
- The scripted form: *"All right, you guys want to know the price of this home. Before I tell you — we are the number one relocation team, give us a call, shoot us a text, send us an email. The price of this house is 1.5. And if you want to see more houses just like it, click this link and I'll see you over there."* (`D3-GS` 00:45:09–00:45:33).
- The claimed effect: *"just doing that fix on your channel alone will change your entire performance of your video"* (`D3-GS` 00:45:33–00:45:38).
- Add a **"kitchen talk"** closing segment — 2–3 minutes back in the kitchen covering community, HOA, taxes, distance to Costco/Target/hospitals — as the vehicle for the re-hook payoffs and the reveal (`D3-GS` 00:43:00–00:43:26). Only raise a topic if it's a *selling* point: don't talk taxes if yours are high; below ~1% is worth featuring (`D3-GS` 00:43:26–00:43:52).

### 8 — The redirect
- The instant the price lands, **point them at the next video or a playlist** (`D3-GS` 00:44:45–00:45:33).
- Verbatim from a second example: *"There's so much more to share about this community, so we are linking a video right here — you're going to want to click this… the price of this home is $969,880. We'll see you in the next video."* Jesse marks this one as **slightly wrong**: she gave the redirect *before* the price, and *"the editor should have clipped that out"* (`D3-GS` 00:54:16–00:55:03).

### The ninth beat he teaches but doesn't count: the payoff
- After the transition, **give an immediate reward** for the ~60–90 seconds already invested: *"she's giving them an instant payoff… everyone loves a good view, everyone loves a nice kitchen"* (`D3-GS` 00:33:08–00:33:31).
- Pick something **non-polarising**. The three things buyers won't compromise on: **kitchen, primary suite, backyard** (`D3-GS` 00:33:31–00:33:43).
- **Start the interior with the kitchen** — *"the kitchen is like the number one thing everyone wants"* (`D3-GS` 00:34:56–00:35:01).

## The Channel Grader

## Traffic quality: relocation vs. local (the whole thesis)

- Relocation buyers are ~**99%** of real-estate business done on YouTube and are worth **4 transactions** each — the referral of their outgoing sale, their purchase, their faster resale, and their referrals (`D1-GS` 00:34:39, `D2-QA` 00:10:51, `D2-GS` 00:24:37).
- Relocators sell **3× faster** and re-transact every **2–3 years** vs. a 7–10 year norm (`D1-GS` 00:36:09).
- Relocation search volume on YouTube is **~10×** Google's for the same terms (300k/mo vs 30k/mo) (`D1-GS` 00:35:34).
- **Nobody searches YouTube for a local agent.** *"No one local is looking to buy and sell real estate on YouTube"* (`D2-QA` 00:09:40). Local content = *"going to Home Depot and handing out your business card"* (`D1-QA` 00:39:35).
- Keep local/in-state traffic **under ~50%** in TX/CA/FL and well under 30% elsewhere (`D1-VIP` 00:49:01).
- Your website is no longer a lead source: *"Before COVID [our website] used to get a lot of closings. After COVID, it got zero"* (`D2-QA` 00:27:42).

## Avatar and the content funnel (Day 1)

**Top of funnel** — search entry, evergreen, takes 30–60 days to start and then runs forever:
"Pros and Cons of Living in [Driver City] in [Year]", "Cost of Living in [Driver City] in [Year]" (`D1-GS` 00:44:28, `D1-VIP` 00:45:17).

**Middle of funnel** — comparison/orientation: "[Driver City] Full Map Tour", "[Driver City]'s Best 5 Suburbs", "Best 3 Master Plan Communities Near [Driver City]" (`D1-GS` 00:47:03).

**Bottom of funnel** — **vlogs and home tours**. This is what YouTube uses for homepage/browse distribution and new-audience testing. Short-lived: home tours are *"brisket — they die a quick death… three to 21 days max"* (`D1-GS` 00:49:51, `D1-VIP` 00:44:19).

- **Content mix 70 / 20 / 10** — core avatar / adjacent audience / experiments (`D1-GS` 00:47:35, `D1-QA` 00:54:56).
- **Audience freshness 70 / 20 / 10** — ~70% of viewers are first-timers, 20% within 5 months, 10% long-term. *You do not need new ideas as often as you think* (`D1-QA` 00:51:18).
- **Driver city rule:** always title to the largest metro, even if you live in a suburb. People relocating to Gilbert only know they're moving to *Phoenix* (`D1-GS` 00:37:51). Suburb form: "Raleigh's best suburb — [Name]" (`D1-QA` 00:31:14); "Top 5 suburbs near Las Vegas" (`D1-VIP` 00:18:03).
- **Price-point rule:** shoot content **one notch above** your average sale — $600–800k market → tour $800k–$1M homes. People love to dream (`D1-GS` 00:50:52).
- **One avatar per channel.** Don't blend relocation + investors (emotional vs. logical buyers) — *"half of your videos are going to go in the toilet on one side and half on the other"* (`D1-VIP` 00:09:28).
- Channel name: **"Living in [Driver City] with [Your Name]"**. Duplicate names don't matter — *"there could be 5 million Living in Boise with Jesse Dau"*; nobody owns a channel name (`D1-QA` 00:24:41, `D2-QA` 00:19:10). The **@handle never took off** — people search your name, not your handle (`D1-QA` 00:32:15).

## Titles

**Home tour formula** (the single most repeated tactic of the week):

> **"Inside / Tour this [emotional adjective] [new] home in / near [Driver City, State]"**

- "Tour this **affordable** new home near Houston, Texas" · "Tour this **luxury** home in Houston, Texas" (`D1-QA` 00:15:53)
- "Tour this **incredibly affordable** house in College Station, Texas" · "Tour this **insanely beautiful** home in College Station" (`D2-QA` 00:28:38)
- Pick the adjective to match **your** avatar — don't copy Jesse's verbatim (`D1-QA` 00:53:55).

**Never put in a title or thumbnail:**
- **A dollar amount.** "What $500,000 gets you in Boise" alienates every other price band — a Bay Area viewer reads $500k as a *"dumpster box"* (`D1-QA` 00:15:07, `D1-VIP` 00:46:20). Say "most affordable" instead.
- **"For sale."** *"'New home for sale' is the most searched keyword on Google for local traffic, so you're just gonna blow up your local traffic"* (`D1-QA` 00:16:33, `D1-GS` 00:53:01).
- **News / hype titles.** "Everything new and coming to [City] in 2026", "Why billion-dollar investors are investing in [City]", "Billion dollar megaplex coming to [City]", "Where old money lives in [City]", "Why Elon Musk moved Tesla to [City]", "Why [company] is building an AI data center in [City]". These pull **95% local** traffic and turn you into a newscaster (`D1-QA` 00:38:04, `D1-VIP` 00:34:55, `D2-GS` 00:21:32).
  - Cautionary tale: **Ian in Austin** ran a channel doing **$30M/yr**, switched to megaplex/Elon-Musk local news content, and **closings went to zero**. He started a fresh channel and was back in leads in **under 30 days** (`D1-VIP` Part 2 00:03:50).

**Also:** delete or retitle any video with big impressions and a nonsense title, and any video whose audience is overwhelmingly local (`D1-QA` 00:37:07).

## Thumbnails

- **Run 3 thumbnails per video** as an A/B/C test. Every time a viewer logs back in, YouTube rotates them — another bite at the apple. *"It's like having three different products in the store"* (`D1-VIP` 00:42:54).
- **Only default to a "winner" at 20%+ variation** — YouTube's own guidance, because YouTube measures **watch time**. TubeBuddy defaults to 3% because it measures **CTR**. If variation is small, keep all three running (`D1-VIP` 00:43:23).
- Native split test: upload screen → thumbnail section → ⋯ → **"Set up a split test"**, up to 3 thumbnails, optionally with different titles (`D1-QA` 00:51:47).
- **AI thumbnails are allowed** — YouTube won't penalise the channel. The only risk is that they *look* AI and never get clicked: *"you got to take all of the fake AI out of the thumbnail"* (`D2-QA` 00:23:45).
- Packaging is secondary to the video: *"You can have subpar thumbnails, subpar titles, but a great video and the video will still do good. But if you have a bad video and great thumbnails and a great title, the video ain't going to do jack"* (`D2-GS` 00:45:54, `D1-QA` 00:39:19).

## Scripting, hooks and retention

**The home-tour retention structure** (`D2-GS` 00:14:17–00:16:43) — the highest-leverage
tactic in the whole challenge:

1. **Never open with the price.** Only ~**3%** of the audience is actively buying right now; leading with a number narrows you to a subset of a subset.
2. **Hook + call-out in under 5 seconds:**
   > *"So you're thinking about moving out here to Boise, Idaho. Well, in this video I'm going to be showing you guys one of the most affordable houses that I found in the entire area. So let's get into it."*
3. **Put the CTA immediately BEFORE the price reveal at the end:**
   > *"All right, so I know you guys all want to know the price of this house. Well, before I tell you that — remember I'm the top agent here in the market, helping people like you relocate every single day. So if you're thinking about moving out here, give me a call, shoot me a text, send me an email. The price of this house is $400,000. And if you want to see more like it, go ahead and click on this link."*
4. This one change turns the "quarter-pipe" retention drop-off into a **flat line to the end of the video**.

**Every video opens by naming the viewer:** *"So you're thinking about moving to [City, State]"* — makes the viewer look in the mirror (`D1-GS` 00:42:23).

**The "undercover call to action"** — weave your lender/partner into the narrative instead of interviewing them. Don't do podcast-style interviews. Give the lender a role, don't let them get into lending weeds, then close soft: *"give us a call, shoot us a text, send us an email — no strings attached, we're not going to force you into any contracts, you don't have to pay us any money"* (`D1-VIP` 00:58:23).

> *"On the YouTube channel we're giving them what they want, but then we're giving them
> the messages of what they need to hear."* — `D1-VIP` 01:00:32

**Slow down and go granular.** Don't say "stainless steel appliances" — talk about the
fridge, oven, stove, dishwasher and sink individually; that's 90 seconds of content you
just skipped. Pretend you're your best friend's eyes and ears. Build **signature details**
you always comment on (Jesse: single-bowl offset-drain sinks; his wife: big drawers) so
returning viewers get an inside joke (`D1-QA` 00:47:38).

**Never interview the builder.** *You* are the character in the show. You can absolutely
narrate construction quality yourself — conditioned vs. unconditioned crawl spaces, 12"
vs. 18" framing offsets, vapor barrier, roof underlayment (`D1-QA` 00:25:25).

### AI and scripts
- **The "AI 20" rule:** AI may do **80%**, human intelligence must do the other **20%** — polishing, proofing, making it sound like you (`D1-QA` 00:04:07).
- YouTube has **built-in AI language and imagery detection**; index too high and it throttles the video (`D1-QA` 00:03:34, `D1-GS` 00:13:44).
- Tells that get you smelled out: "five things no other agent is telling you", em-dashes, "this city's a vibe" (`D1-QA` 00:05:11).
- > *"AI takes about three paragraphs to communicate what I can say in one sentence. So I have a massive advantage."* — `D1-QA` 00:06:00
- > *"If you're reading an AI script, my AI avatar is actually better than you — it's more consistent and it can create more volume."* — `D2-QA` 00:12:19
- **The moat:** an AI avatar can replicate a talking head. It cannot replicate a vlog or a walk-through home tour. *"AI can't disrupt truth. AI can't disrupt demonstration of content."* (`D1-QA` 00:36:57, `D2-QA` 00:11:44).
- Framing for the whole era: *"We are moving away from an attention-based economy into a trust-based economy."* (`D1-VIP` 00:56:36).

## Filming, gear and format

### The home-tour shot sequence (Day 3, condensed)

1. **Outside, framed on the house** (or the backyard if your exteriors are repetitive) — call-out, then stacked hooks. Under 30 seconds.
2. **Cut.** Restart at the front door. *"Come on in, Ken."*
3. **Immediate payoff** — the view, the kitchen, the backyard. Something nobody dislikes.
4. **Kitchen first**, then work the house. Zigzag side-to-side so the operator can hold position.
5. **Granular narration** throughout, with re-hooks planted for the closing segment.
6. **First spoken CTA around the 4-minute mark**, then snap straight back to the house.
7. **"Kitchen talk"** — 2–3 minutes back in the kitchen on community, HOA, taxes, amenities; pay off the re-hooks.
8. **CTA → price reveal → redirect** to the next video or playlist. In that order.

**Walk the house before you film.** Jesse draws a map on paper, marks his **avatar hot-spots**, and plans visual guide-points through the house (`D3-GS` 00:41:56–00:42:17).

### The vlog / community tour (Day 4)

Jesse's preferred structure, which he says he now coaches Rachel on (`D4-GS` 00:49:29–00:51:24):

1. **Open at an "anchor location"** — the nearest big-box landmark, typically **Costco**. Do the intro there.
2. Use the anchor to answer the unspoken question — *"I'm standing in front of Costco and you're saying you're in Middleton, but I thought Middleton was in the country? Well, I just wanted to show you how far it is to drive."*
3. **Tell them the itinerary up front**: community one, community two, community three — each a different *type* (low-maintenance/retiree, one-acre with horses, affordable).
4. Drive each one, covering **what it's like to live there and how far it is**.
5. **Then the little downtown pocket**, and then **where people actually go**.
6. **Be real, not promotional** — *"we're not trying to say oh this is the greatest place in the world… we're just showing what it's like to live there."* He volunteers that the Treasure Valley is short on specialty doctors as an example of saying the unflattering thing (`D4-GS` 00:50:43–00:51:24).

**Map tours are the sleeper format.** *"Map tours are one of the hottest videos you could ever make for your channel; it's one of the highest intent videos as well"* — and they earn browse/suggested *and* search (`D4-GS` 00:51:48–00:51:58).

**Channel trailer:** you can set **two** — one for subscribers, one for non-subscribers. Jesse's pick for the non-subscriber slot is a **map tour**, because it orients a stranger to the whole area (`D4-GS` 00:51:58–00:52:31).

### Shoot-day limits and cadence

- **One to two home tours a day is the ceiling.** *"After like three videos, in my opinion, it's game over… they all start blending together."* Jesse compares it to showing a buyer 3–5 houses (`D3-GS` 00:55:34–00:56:09).
- This is the **practical counterweight to the "48 videos in one session"** figure from Day 2 — that number is for **talking-head** content filmed in one place, not for tours. Rachel's actual tour cadence is **8 videos every other week** (`D4-GS` 00:47:22–00:47:31), described elsewhere as **filming two days a month and releasing two videos a week** (`D4-GS` 00:32:22–00:32:28).
- Rachel's release pattern shifted mid-2026: **two home tours per week, or a vlog + a home tour**, with the **best video on Wednesday**. They dropped the weekly talking head because *"we kind of burned our audience out of like the talking heads — the viewership was significantly low."* Jesse flags it as an **8-week experiment**, not settled doctrine (`D3-VIP` 00:50:21–00:51:16).
- Even Rachel hits the wall: *"Jesse, after I film like three videos my brain is starting to turn to mush"* (`D4-GS` 00:11:04–00:11:09).

### Gear and format

- **Shoot 1080p / 60fps. Do not shoot 4K.** Storage burden, slow transfers to offshore editors, and no benefit — *"the only reason you would ever need 4K is if you were going to start playing these videos at the movie theater"* (`D2-GS` 00:19:43).
- **Current kit:** DJI **Osmo Pocket 3** (Creator Combo) + **DJI wireless mic**. Optional drone. That's it — the old GoPro-7-plus-boom-mic rig is *"way too cumbersome"* (`D1-QA` 00:20:32).
- **Green screens are played out.** Shoot talking heads on location — outdoors, in new construction, in front of a house — not against a blank white wall (`D1-QA` 00:34:40).
- If you could only ever make one format: **vlogs**, because a vlog can absorb home tours, communities and talking-head information. *"King of the vlog is king of the city"* (`D1-QA` 00:36:41).
- Production value is not the constraint: *"I literally have a whiteboard and a freaking painted wall behind me and I've been doing this for 10 years — if you deliver good information it doesn't matter what it looks like."* Audio quality does matter (`D1-VIP` 00:57:28). Day 3 sharpens this: *"at the end of the day the information is what matters the most — it's not the house… if you have good content it doesn't matter the quality of the video, people are going to listen"* (`D3-VIP` 00:50:55–00:51:16).
- **Bulk film.** Jesse's wife shoots **48 videos in one session** and does not change clothes between them — 70% of viewers have never seen you before (`D2-QA` 00:03:05). See the shoot-day ceiling above for how this applies to tours.
- Time budget: talking-head ~1 hour to make; vlog tours 2–4 hours (`D2-GS` 00:08:26, `D3-GS` 00:07:56).
- Storage: a **2TB** external drive with four folders — raw footage / episodes / generic B-roll / photos — is plenty (`D2-QA` 00:20:25).
- Getting into homes: call the listing agent, ask about the property, let them talk, then *"when I'm in your property, would you mind if I shot some content?"* Afterwards send them the impressions and watch-hours and put their name in the description. ~90% of homes they film go pending the week the video drops (`D1-QA` 00:25:25–00:30:39).
- **Never use footage you don't own.** Two clients were caught: one used ~3 hours of another creator's drone footage, got the channel shut down, and had to **wire the owner ~$14,000** to get it restored. Another's videographer lifted community photos on the theory that an unmonetized channel was fair game (`D4-GS` 00:54:38–00:55:43).
- **Strike policy:** one strike is a warning, two puts you in a **30-day penalty box**, three removes you from YouTube permanently. Uploading the **same video to two channels** can earn one (`D4-GS` 00:54:13–00:54:33).

## ⭐ The 90-day plan (Day 4 — "action and profit")

Delivered off a whiteboard; also printed in the paid challenge workbook (`D4-GS` 00:07:11).
The three blocks (`D4-GS` 00:07:42, 00:10:04, 00:17:36):

| Block | Focus | What you actually do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Days 1–30** | **Build the channel** | Channel art, name, description. Plan the filming schedule and upload sequence — how often you film, how many a week. Bank videos. Then start publishing. |
| **Days 31–60** | **Validation, consistency, learn from testing** | Read your own retention graphs and metrics. Watch playbacks on a TV and take edit notes. The Grader needs ~28 days of data before it says anything useful. |
| **Days 61–90** | **Build algorithm trust** | Work the limiters *in order*: consistency → AVD → TV views → browse+suggested → no Shorts/no ads. Then "real-time power" starts to kick in. |

### Rules inside the plan

- **Do not publish until you have videos banked.** Minimum **4**; Jesse's recommendation is **10**. When they ran the done-for-you agency they wouldn't release anything until **10 were complete** (`D4-GS` 00:08:04–00:08:26).
  - The reason is attrition, not polish: an agent films one, uploads it, and *"then they're like, oh my gosh, I can't do this 10 times over — like, this is too much. I'm like, exactly."* (`D4-GS` 00:08:20–00:08:41).
  - Second reason: **life happens.** *"When you have four videos that are edited and you get sick or you get COVID… and you're out for 30 days, you've filmed enough content… It's not a matter of if, it's a matter of when."* (`D4-GS` 00:28:06–00:28:35).
- **Give your editor a 14-day buffer.** That was their own internal SLA — submit, then 14 days to edit, review with the agent, correct, upload. *"If you guys are working with an editor, please just give them 14 days — it'll save your guys's life so much misery"* (`D4-GS` 00:08:41–00:09:08).
- **Cadence: one video a week is the floor, two is better.** *"If you can't commit to one video a week you're in the wrong room"* (`D4-GS` 00:40:47–00:40:59). Every other week measurably underperforms (`D4-GS` 00:41:42–00:41:55).
- **"20-10" (also said as "2010 ATS")** — Jackson's rule: **20 videos in 10 weeks**, *then* read your analytics and traffic sources, because they take ~10 weeks to mean anything (`D4-GS` 00:24:06–00:24:23, 00:32:52–00:33:02).
- **Never seed your own views.** Do not send new videos to friends, family, past clients or other agents, and don't watch them yourself — *"let YouTube build it organically."* A relaunched client channel had to be **scrapped and started over a second time** purely because they'd shared the first five videos with their sphere (`D4-GS` 00:24:37–00:25:53).
- **Don't start a second channel** until the first is maxed. The restaurant analogy: opening the second one before the first runs on autopilot halves your time and sinks both. Agents running two channels typically do **90% of business on one and 10% on the other** (`D4-GS` 00:33:53–00:34:48).

### The first videos to make, in order

From the whiteboard (`D4-GS` 00:18:35–00:19:27):

1. **"Pros and Cons of Living in [Driver City]"** — *"the number one video we should all have on our channel."*
2. **"The Real Cost of Living in [Driver City] in [Year]"** — and get ahead of the calendar; film the 2027 version late in 2026 to *"beat everyone to the punch."*
3. **"The Honest Truth of Living in [City, State] After [N] Years"** — no sugar-coating.

**Use your tenure either way.** Forty years in the market is credibility; thirty days in the market is *"I just relocated here 30 days ago, I'm just like you — here are the things I wish I'd known."* Both work; *"you have to fight with what you got"* (`D4-GS` 00:19:27–00:19:58).

### The lead math behind the plan

- **~16% of YouTube leads close.** Of leads, **~50% should convert to a Zoom or a real scheduled phone consult**, and **~32% of those Zooms convert** (`D4-GS` 00:22:27–00:22:59).
- So **100 leads a year → ~16 closings**, and 100 leads a year is **about two leads a week**. *"That's all you need. Everyone thinks it's got to be this crazy number."* (`D4-GS` 00:22:59–00:23:21).
- **The 16% is not universal — it is per-channel.** Jesse's Boise channel converts at **16%**; his Houston channel at **4%** (`D3-VIP` 00:18:33–00:18:54). The ~4% figure recorded from Day 1 is the Houston number, so the two are **not in conflict** — they bracket the range.
- Jesse has gone **30 days without a single lead** in a year he still did **$1M GCI** (`D4-GS` 00:23:21–00:23:26).

### Handling the psychology

- The five stages, borrowed from Alex Hormozi: **uninformed optimism → informed pessimism → the valley of despair → informed optimism → success.** Chasing something new returns you to stage one; *"if you're the type of person that is consistently starting something new, you are just consistently living in that vicious cycle"* (`D4-GS` 00:04:13–00:05:33).
- **Don't chase perfection** — *"they say it takes a hundred videos"* (`D4-GS` 00:11:45–00:11:53).
- **Burnout is a purpose problem, not a workload problem:** *"the reason you did this to begin with is no longer applying… you no longer have meaning behind why you're doing it"* (`D4-GS` 00:26:14–00:26:35).
- **Don't argue with commenters.** Hide user from channel; do the same to locals. And pre-emptively **block trigger words in channel settings** — cuss words, plus anything off-avatar (Jesse blocks "rent", "rental", and locally-charged political terms) so they never appear (`D4-GS` 00:12:35–00:17:36).
- **"Indexing" is a trap.** If a video gets more comments/likes than views, YouTube pushes it at more of the same reactive audience. Jesse's COVID-era Oculus video went viral this way and brought nothing but abuse (`D4-GS` 00:13:10–00:14:47).
- **Avoid "Avoid moving to [City]" and other negative/clickbait framings** — they pull locals, and locals may report you for fair-housing violations. Jesse worked with NAR attorneys over exactly this; they sided with him (saying a neighborhood is walkable is not steering), but the hassle is real (`D4-GS` 00:15:21–00:16:04).

### Reading a channel in YouTube Studio (Day 4 VIP method)

Switch Studio to **Advanced mode**, then work these four reports (`D4-VIP` 00:47:05–00:53:50):

1. **Traffic sources** — is browse+suggested carrying it, or search?
2. **Audience → watch behaviour** — new / casual / regular split.
3. **Content → "videos growing your audience"** — which videos actually recruit.
4. **Search keywords** — *"if all of these other cities are up top and we don't even get to the driver city till the bottom of that page, that tells me YouTube's not showing your channel to anybody that's searching out Houston information"* (`D4-VIP` 00:29:14–00:29:41).

**The impressions-vs-views cascade.** Sort videos by impressions; impressions and views
should fall together roughly linearly. *"Say you have a video that has like double the
amount of impressions of any other video on that list, but the views are significantly
lower — it tells me I either have a titling or a thumbnail issue"* (`D4-VIP` 00:27:54–00:28:15).

**Fixing old videos:** re-titling anything **more than ~2 weeks old** generally won't move
it. The exception is evergreen search content — pros & cons, cost of living — which is
worth reworking (`D4-VIP` 00:33:46–00:34:08). There's a whole paid module ("L2") on
title/thumbnail testing (`D4-VIP` 00:33:28).

**Cleansing an existing channel takes time:** a brand-new channel has no baggage, but an
existing one needs *"a cleansing cycle to get the data cleaned up… that could take you 10
weeks"* (`D4-VIP` 00:08:52–00:09:11).

**Three-way title tests.** For one attendee's Scottsdale tour Jesse proposed testing three
titles against three thumbnails, varying both the benefit and the geo-modifier
(`D4-VIP` 00:43:58–00:44:04):
*"Tour this luxury remodeled home in Old Town Scottsdale with mountain views"* ·
*"Tour this new home with insane views in Scottsdale Arizona"* ·
*"Tour this remodeled home in Old Town Scottsdale with roof deck"*.

**Consistent opening music.** *"I always say run your HGTV music, so it's always starting
out the same"* — one attendee pair had custom branding music made for their tours
(`D4-VIP` 00:45:56–00:46:01).

Diagnostic example — a real audited channel (`D1-VIP` 00:29:55–00:36:55): 72.8% new /
24.1% casual / **3.1% returning**; AVD **3:47**; traffic 34% search / 20% shorts / 17%
browse / 9% suggested. Verdict: delete the Shorts, stop the ads, and re-title everything.

## ⭐ The launch / relaunch video sequence (Day 4 VIP — the most actionable thing in the week)

Given to an attendee (Joe) rebooting a stale channel, but Jesse says it is the sequence
*"in the playbook and in the Challenge Bot"* and the one he'd use for a brand-new channel
too (`D4-VIP` 00:35:41–00:37:38). Two videos a week, eight weeks:

| Week | **Wednesday** | **Saturday** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cost of Living in [Driver City] | Full vlog tour |
| 2 | Pros and Cons of Living in [Driver City] | Full vlog tour |
| 3 | Map tour | Full vlog tour |
| 4 | Best 5 neighborhoods in [Driver City] | Full vlog tour |
| 5–8 | **Home tours** | Full vlog tour |

> *"Weeks one, two, three, four — I'd do these four to start, and then weeks five, six,
> seven, eight I'm switching to home tours, because these are all that you need for the top
> of funnel to start… The vlog tours though, every single week — the vlog tours are just
> like dynamite to a channel, in a good way, especially if you're trying to activate browse
> and suggested."* — `D4-VIP` 00:36:50–00:37:38 *(stutter artifacts removed)*

### ⚠️ A real refinement: home tours are the *engine*, not the *foundation*

This is the one place Days 3–5 genuinely sharpen the Days 1–2 story, so read it carefully
rather than treating it as a contradiction:

> *"If you told me, what is the most important videos to make for your channel — pros and
> cons and cost of living are my top two videos. Then I'm saying vlog tour of your biggest
> city, then I'm saying map tour. So those are the four most important videos every agent
> should have on their channel. And then I'm moving to — and you guys notice I even say home
> tour — that is **not** the most important video to have on your channel as a mandate,
> because home tours die a fast death. You're just going to keep rotating through."*
> — `D4-VIP` 00:39:26

Reconciled with the rest of the week:

- **Home tours and vlogs remain what activates browse/suggested.** Day 4's general session is unambiguous: *"if you want to activate the algorithm and get browse and suggested, the videos that pop… it's vlogs and home tours, that's it"* (`D4-GS` 00:49:19–00:49:29).
- **But home tours are consumable, not foundational.** They die in 3–21 days (`D1-GS` 00:49:51), so they are the *rotating* fuel, not the permanent asset base.
- **The four permanent assets** are pros & cons, cost of living, a full vlog tour of the driver city, and a map tour.
- **Vlogs are the one format to run every single week.** *"Dynamite to a channel."*

So the correct reading is: **build the four evergreen assets first, run vlogs weekly
forever, and rotate home tours through continuously** — not "home tours are less
important."

### How to actually film a vlog tour: shoot the intro LAST

A concrete process refinement, stated explicitly (`D4-VIP` 00:39:56–00:41:02):

> *"We start from our key anchor point — so if it's like Costco or whatever… we're 15
> minutes away from our first neighborhood we're going to go tour. Then we hit three
> neighborhoods, three different communities where people can buy houses, and then we're
> going to go to the downtown area… We never shoot the intro. I said starting at the key
> anchor point — we never do the intro until we film the video, because we want to know what
> we talk about after we filmed our video. So film all of your pieces, and then whatever the
> anchor point is is actually where you're going to end, and that's where you're going to do
> your intro. I know it sounds backwards, but that's just the way you have to do it, because
> what happens every time is you film your vlog tour and you're like, oh shoot, I wish I

## ⭐ Day 5 — the fireside chat with Rachel Dau (the production-workflow session)

**What's in the recording.** Day 5 is a single 2h24m file: raffle and housekeeping
(`D5-RAFFLE` 00:00:25–00:04:48, ~4 min), the **fireside chat with Rachel**
(`D5-FS` 00:04:49–01:03:14, ~58 min), then open Q&A (`D5-QA` 01:03:15–end, ~81 min).
Only about **6% of the runtime is promotional** — the raffle, a coaching mention and
thank-yous. The rest is substance. This is the most useful session in the week for a **solo
agent doing her own filming**.

### ⭐ Her answer to "front door or linger outside?"

This is the question Jesse deliberately refused to pre-brief her on (`D3-GS` 00:31:18), and
she answers it at `D5-FS` 00:23:48. **She decides at the curb, and the decision is: is this
a lifestyle video or a house video?**

> *"So we get to the house. I look at the curb and I look around — is this a lifestyle
> video or is this the house? When I say lifestyle: is this house on a pond and does it
> have turf, so they can be a snowbird, this is a lock-and-leave situation? Or is it an
> acreage with a white picket fence? Or is this a house in a community that they're all
> kind of cookie-cutter and they look the same? So I kind of know right out the gate."*
> — `D5-FS` 00:23:48

> *"This has a double RV bay, a white picket fence, you can have a cow, a horse and
> chickens — and I'm going to **tease the front of this house more, or engage more
> outside**, because we're selling this community and lifestyle here. Or is this a house I
> need to **get into quickly** and really go for selling the safety, the nearby amenities,
> having conversations more about the community, and **not linger around** as much."*
> — `D5-FS` 00:24:18

**The rule, distilled:** *the property's distinguishing feature is outside* → linger and
tease outside. *The property is one of many similar homes* → get inside fast and sell the
community instead.

### The rest of her in-house method (`D5-FS` 00:24:18–00:27:32)

- **Scout while you turn the lights on.** She looks for things her specific audience will love — *"is there a gun room… a gun safe room?"*, or for a mother of several children *"a little spot in the house where she can kind of get away and eat her chocolate."* She's hunting for **jokes and sweet spots**, not features.
- **Announce your re-hooks out loud, then defer them.** *"I do say to them all throughout the house, 'I know you guys want to know about X, Y and Z' — I almost interrupt myself and say 'we're going to get there.' So I know what I'm going to re-hook throughout the entire video."*
- **Immediate payoff, then downplay it.** *"We have a payoff right away — I bring them straight into the house and go straight to the kitchen, because everyone wants to see the kitchen… or I go straight to the backyard that has a show-stopping view… so they get an immediate reward. And then I make it kind of sound like this isn't the coolest part of the house, and I keep re-hooking that it's coming up."*
- **Move fast through boring rooms and talk harder.** *"'I know you really want to see it, but real quick we need to show you these other bedrooms.' I know those are boring to look at, so that's where the strong conversation and talking points come in, and I move very very fast."*
- **The mental model:** *"within the video it's like **30 little micro videos**… there's not much to see, so this needs to be much to say."*

### Her shoot day (`D5-FS` 00:17:27–00:19:27)

- **Up at 4:30am.** *"I get up at 4:30 in the morning and get ready and we start filming early, and we film until one to three o'clock, and we just film one video after another back to back. And I've got it all lined up and ready to go before I show up — I know where we're filming."*
- **The houses are lined up in advance**, by Jesse (`D5-FS` 00:18:03).
- **The prep happens in the hairdresser's chair.** *"It takes me a very long time to do my hair, I'm not good at it, I hate doing it. I can get my hair done start to finish in less than an hour — and while I'm sitting in the chair I am **calculating the property taxes on every house that I'm going to film**, and I'm reviewing, reminding myself of the highlights about the community. And I even write in my notes **the same things that I say in every video** so that I don't forget… I have a bullet point just so I can remind myself. I'm repeating the same conversations over and over again, and doing that before I film helps me be **warmed up, so that I'm not reshooting content over and over again — I'm just able to do everything naturally in one take, typically.**"* (`D5-FS` 00:18:30–00:19:27)
- **Bullet points, never a script.** One take is the norm. **Nerves and stage fright are never discussed** — her answer to the problem is preparation.
- Jesse's adjacent tip: keep a **15-minute time tracker** for a few days and find where time goes that doesn't drive revenue (`D5-FS` 00:18:09).

> ⚠️ **Volume: her practice exceeds his advice.** Jesse's stated ceiling is **1–2 home tours
> a day** (`D3-GS` 00:55:34). Rachel films **8 videos across two shoot days a month** — about
> **4 a day**, on a 4:30am-to-3pm day, after years of practice and with a camera operator and
> a pre-booked route. Treat 1–2 as the number for someone starting out and 4 as what a
> practised, fully-prepped operator can sustain. The gap is **not** reconciled on air.

### Don't put other people on camera

> *"My audience really leans in on my expertise, and so **bringing in other people can kind
> of make it look like you're not the expert — they are.** So I'm really careful about

### Getting into other agents' listings (`D5-FS` 00:33:08–00:36:33)

- **99.99% of what she films is other agents' listings** — she doesn't list much herself.
- **Credit the brokerage** in the description (an Idaho requirement; check your own state).
- **For a listing agent whose inventory you want to keep filming, name them and build a "we're a team" expectation** so buyers don't try to cut you out: *"I know if someone shows up saying 'I found this house on YouTube', that she's going to redirect them right back to my agent Nate… I want to set the expectation for the people watching my channel that they're going to be working with them and us together. We're a team that way. They don't feel like they can go straight to the listing agent and get a better deal."* She names the agent warmly on camera — *"I love this woman"* — so the client arrives already predisposed to like her.

- **Never imply someone else's listing is yours.** *"I never try and make something look like my own listing. I think that's awful… people could care less if your listing house is out there when they're relocating. They care what your heart is like and your values way more than if you're a listing agent in the area. So — the less you talk about yourself and your production, probably the better."*

## Lead conversion and the business behind it

- **One relocation buyer = 4 transactions** (their outgoing sale referral, their purchase, their faster resale, their referrals) (`D2-GS` 00:24:37).
- **Referral income is the quiet engine.** Jesse pulled three uncashed referral checks out of his mailbox that week — **$9,000, $11,000 and ~$11,500** (`D2-GS` 00:25:29). Referrals net better than local deals: no transaction-coordination fee, no drive time.
- **Assumptive-close referral script** — when a relocation client still has a home to sell, assume they're using your partner in that market, then sell the partner on service (communication, 2-week post-closing occupancy, cleaners, handyman touch-ups). Result: they sign a 3.5–4% listing agreement there while you hold a **5% BBA** here (`D2-QA` 00:07:08).
- Barrett (Austin): **$100k GCI months, capped at 8 Zoom calls a week** — the Zoom call, not lead volume, is the throttle (`D2-GS` 01:00:41).
- YouTube leads don't negotiate: *"I tell them it's 3% plus a $350 transaction fee and they're all in. There's no convincing."* (`D1-GS` 00:55:38).
- **Retargeting stack:** YouTube channel → website with a relocation-guide lead magnet → Meta pixel → retarget visitors at **$10/day** (`D2-QA` 00:26:01, `D1-VIP` 00:28:07).
- Attribute each inbound lead to whatever video is live that day, so you can see which content actually pays (`D1-GS` 00:58:31).
- The buyer demographic skews old: YouTube's average buying age is **over 50**, and Jesse cites ~**80%** of buyers as 50+ (`D1-VIP` 00:54:14).

## Notable Q&A answers

- **Multiple channels?** No second channel until you've maxed the first — a fully-worked single channel tops out near **$1.5M GCI** (Level 8 of their L1–L8 value ladder) (`D1-QA` 00:11:15).
- **When to actually restart a channel:** if it's diluted — returning viewers near zero, ~100% new audience, ~90% local traffic — start fresh. You can reuse relocation videos, **but not on both channels; YouTube will strike you for duplicates** (`D1-VIP` Part 2 00:02:54–00:03:25).
- **Tags/hashtags?** Use them, but they're worth roughly **1–2 views** each. Never use a competitor's name (`D1-QA` 00:38:58, `D2-QA` 00:04:28).
- **Private/unlisted videos?** No algorithmic effect — **unless an unlisted video sits in a playlist**. Remove them from playlists (`D2-QA` 00:05:15).
- **Is 30 minutes too long?** *"There's no such thing as a too long video. There is such thing as a too long, unengaging video."* But not on a brand-new channel — no trust built yet (`D2-QA` 00:05:37).
- **TubeBuddy vs vidIQ?** TubeBuddy, with caveats (use it for driver-city search volume and to compare your last-5-video average against the weakest video in a term's top 20). vidIQ leans on vanity metrics: *"I've never seen somebody absolutely destroying it on YouTube that uses vidIQ at a high level"* (`D1-QA` 00:42:46).
- **YouTube Live?** Generally no — lives pull local traffic and die faster than home tours; many agents have killed channels with them. Run the event on Zoom registration instead (`D1-QA` 00:59:34).
- **Same channel name as a competitor?** Fine — add your own name. But make clear you're *not* affiliated with them; Jesse has seen clients sign a BBA with the wrong "Living in" agent thinking they were the same team (`D2-QA` 00:19:10).
- **Where to find editors?** Fiverr, online job boards, local Facebook groups (`D2-QA` 00:24:27).
- **Tom Ferry's "Google Trifecta"?** Google→YouTube data flow is real but *"external traffic is very minimal."* Jesse's preferred trifecta is YouTube + website + Meta retargeting (`D2-QA` 00:24:43).
- **Are LLMs a traffic source now?** Increasingly — Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity and Claude all cite YouTube videos, and *"everything is signal data"* (`D2-QA` 00:25:25).
- **Vanity metrics in perspective:** Jesse's #1 channel has **10M+ views** that earned **≤$35k/yr in ad revenue** — and **$3.5M in commissions** (`D1-VIP` Part 2 00:00:08).
