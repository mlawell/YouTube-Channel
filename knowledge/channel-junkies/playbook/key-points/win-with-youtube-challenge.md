## ⭐ The Seven Limiter Framework (Day 2 — the core of the program)

A "waterfall": each limiter unlocks the next, and **every limiter carries a weighted
score** (`D2-GS` 00:06:26, 00:21:04). Full list as recapped in one breath at
`D2-QA` 00:17:22–00:17:55:

| # | Limiter | Target | Where to read it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Consistency** | ≥ **1 video/week** (2 is better) | Studio → Content (upload cadence) |
| 2 | **Average view duration** | ≥ **7 minutes** | Analytics → per-video + channel AVD |
| 3 | **TV / big-screen views** | ≥ **50%** (top channels hit 70%) | Analytics → Audience → device type |
| 4 | **Browse + Suggested traffic** | > **70%** — *the most heavily weighted limiter* | Analytics → Reach → Traffic sources |
| 5 | **No ads** | Zero paid traffic pointed at your videos | n/a (a decision, not a metric) |
| 6 | **No Shorts** | Zero Shorts on the channel | Studio → Content → filter Shorts |
| 7 | **Portfolio depth** | **15+ videos with 50+ views** in the past 7 days | Channel Grader |

> *"Every limiter has a weighted measurement. The number one measurement on any channel
> to get the best performance is having your browse and suggested traffic above 70."*
> — `D2-GS` 00:21:04

**Payoff when all seven are met:** *"more views from the right watcher… more watch time
by the highest intent viewers… more credibility… more leads and more profit"*
(`D2-GS` 00:34:00).

### 1 — Consistency
- One video a week is the floor; **two per week** is the working standard (`D2-GS` 00:08:02, `D1-GS` 00:56:20).
- **Never bulk-upload.** Film in bulk, release on schedule — *"YouTube is a pipeline game… if you overload it, all of those videos just step on each other"* (`D2-QA` 00:19:02).
- Keep **"two in the queue"** — two videos already uploaded and scheduled (`D2-GS` 00:10:58).
- Best days: **Wednesday + Saturday**. Saturday is when relocating families have time; Wednesday has almost no competition and feeds viewers into Saturday (`D2-GS` 00:11:11, `D1-GS` 00:57:45). Post times: Sat **9am local**, Wed **5pm local** (`D2-QA` 00:04:05).
- Put your **best** video on Wednesday — discovered by accident on Jesse's wife's channel (`D1-VIP` 00:44:49).
- Avoid snow/seasonal footage — YouTube treats it as time-sensitive and it fizzles (`D2-GS` 00:10:29).
- Skip the **Premiere** feature: *"I don't think it does anything"* (`D2-GS` 00:11:07, `D1-QA` 01:03:13).

### 2 — Average view duration ≥ 7 minutes
- The math: a good video is watched **~28%** through, so a **21–22 minute** video lands just under 7 minutes AVD (`D2-GS` 00:13:16).
- Lengths: **talking head / informational 15–20 min**; **home tours and vlogs 18–28 min** (`D2-GS` 00:13:49, `D1-GS` 00:56:25).
- Climb in **micro-steps** — if a 12-minute video yields 3–4 min AVD, go to 13, then 14 (`D2-GS` 00:16:51).
- Voiceover-over-B-roll rarely clears 10 min AVD; a live filmed walk-through does (`D1-QA` 00:48:32).
- Big drops in AVD/CTR when a video breaks out are normal — YouTube is testing it on a wider audience (`D1-VIP` 00:39:09).
- > *"It isn't that the consumer has a short attention span. It's just that we make a lot of bad videos."* — `D2-GS` 00:14:17

### 3 — TV views ≥ 50%
- YouTube is the #1 TV network — Neil Mohan at TV Connected Con: *"We're not trying to be TV. TV is trying to be us."* (`D2-GS` 00:17:44).
- TV viewers watch ~2× longer, subscribe more, and are the ones who call you: *"my family and I watch your guys' show every single Saturday morning"* (`D2-GS` 00:19:07).
- Mobile as your #1 device = a symptom of Shorts, ads, or short searchable content. Correct order is **TV → tablet/desktop → mobile** (`D2-GS` 00:17:13).
- TV % is an **output** of limiters 1–2, not a thing you act on directly.
- Watch your own videos back **on a TV** to see what viewers see (`D2-GS` 00:18:18).

### 4 — Browse + Suggested > 70% (highest weight)
- **Browse** = homepage distribution. **Suggested** = "up next". Together they are what Studio labels **YouTube Recommended** — that *is* the algorithm. **Search is not** (`D2-GS` 00:26:42).
- Search is only **10–15%** of YouTube traffic (`D1-GS` 00:39:23, `D1-QA` 00:41:25).
- > *"If you're focused on search traffic, you're literally fighting the algorithm."* — `D2-GS` 00:27:13
- Below 70% means **6–10× viewership growth is sitting on the table**; browse+suggested impressions should be **6–10× your search impressions** (`D2-GS` 00:27:47, `D1-VIP` 00:37:44).
- Jesse's guess: browse+suggested is the #1 limiter on **~90%** of channels in the room (`D2-GS` 00:20:48).
- **The trap:** you can score >70% browse and still get zero leads if that traffic is *local*. News-style titles pass the metric and fail the business (`D2-GS` 00:21:22).

### 5 & 6 — No ads, no Shorts
- Ads and Shorts do identical damage: tank AVD, collapse browse/suggested, flip your device mix to mobile. *"Ads and shorts are the two worst things you can do for your channel"* (`D1-VIP` 00:26:41).
- > *"No one is going to watch you for seven minutes and then watch your competitor for 30 seconds on your channel and then choose them over you. That is absurd."* — `D2-GS` 00:28:38
- > *"Imagine making the most beautiful recipe. And then at the very end, someone dumps like a gallon of ammonia in there. That's what putting shorts on your YouTube channel [is]."* — `D2-GS` 00:30:53
- **Delete existing Shorts — do not unlist.** *"150% yes… just delete and keep deleting until there's no more Shorts left"* (`D1-QA` 00:07:30; `D2-GS` 00:30:44; `D1-VIP` 01:00:49).
- **Ad revenue ≠ running ads.** Leave monetization ON. Jesse asked YouTube directly: monetized vs not makes **0% difference** to distribution or leads (`D1-QA` 00:52:53, `D2-GS` 00:28:09).
- Don't clip long-form into Reels/Shorts to "feed" the channel either — check how many Shorts viewers ever returned: *"I will guarantee you it is probably zero"* (`D2-QA` 00:13:48).

### 7 — 15+ videos with 50+ views in the past 7 days
- Proof YouTube is pushing your **whole portfolio**, not just the newest upload (`D2-GS` 00:31:27).
- Real numbers: Jesse's wife's channel **36**; the Houston channel up to **60**; the floor case — a client with **1,000 subs and ~200 views/video** still hits **15** (`D2-GS` 00:32:51–00:33:33).
- Run the Grader **Sat–Mon**; weekend viewing settles the number (`D2-GS` 00:31:36).

## The Channel Grader

- **`channeljunkies.com/grader`** — spelled out on air (`D2-QA` 00:17:00). Free, no credit card, **read-only Google-authorized** via the YouTube API (`D2-QA` 00:17:07, `D2-GS` 00:31:53).
- Scores **1–100** against the seven limiters and names your single biggest limiter (`D2-GS` 00:03:11).
- Treat the score as a **north star, not a grade**: climb 50 → 60 → 70 → 80. Agents have gone **30 → 70 in under 60 days, and leads started at 70** (`D2-QA` 00:18:14, `D2-GS` 00:03:11).

## Traffic quality: relocation vs. local (the whole thesis)

- Relocation buyers are ~**99%** of real-estate business done on YouTube and are worth **4 transactions** each — the referral of their outgoing sale, their purchase, their faster resale, and their referrals (`D1-GS` 00:34:39, `D2-QA` 00:10:51, `D2-GS` 00:24:37).
- Relocators sell **3× faster** and re-transact every **2–3 years** vs. a 7–10 year norm (`D1-GS` 00:36:09).
- Relocation search volume on YouTube is **~10×** Google's for the same terms (300k/mo vs 30k/mo) (`D1-GS` 00:35:34).
- **Nobody searches YouTube for a local agent.** *"No one local is looking to buy and sell real estate on YouTube"* (`D2-QA` 00:09:40). Local content = *"going to Home Depot and handing out your business card"* (`D1-QA` 00:39:35).
- Keep local/in-state traffic **under ~50%** in TX/CA/FL and well under 30% elsewhere (`D1-VIP` 00:49:01).
- Your website is no longer a lead source: *"Before COVID [our website] used to get a lot of closings. After COVID, it got zero"* (`D2-QA` 00:27:42).

## Avatar and the content funnel (Day 1)

**Top of funnel** — search entry, evergreen, takes 30–60 days to start and then runs forever:
"Pros and Cons of Living in [Driver City] in [Year]", "Cost of Living in [Driver City] in [Year]" (`D1-GS` 00:44:28, `D1-VIP` 00:45:17).

**Middle of funnel** — comparison/orientation: "[Driver City] Full Map Tour", "[Driver City]'s Best 5 Suburbs", "Best 3 Master Plan Communities Near [Driver City]" (`D1-GS` 00:47:03).

**Bottom of funnel** — **vlogs and home tours**. This is what YouTube uses for homepage/browse distribution and new-audience testing. Short-lived: home tours are *"brisket — they die a quick death… three to 21 days max"* (`D1-GS` 00:49:51, `D1-VIP` 00:44:19).

- **Content mix 70 / 20 / 10** — core avatar / adjacent audience / experiments (`D1-GS` 00:47:35, `D1-QA` 00:54:56).
- **Audience freshness 70 / 20 / 10** — ~70% of viewers are first-timers, 20% within 5 months, 10% long-term. *You do not need new ideas as often as you think* (`D1-QA` 00:51:18).
- **Driver city rule:** always title to the largest metro, even if you live in a suburb. People relocating to Gilbert only know they're moving to *Phoenix* (`D1-GS` 00:37:51). Suburb form: "Raleigh's best suburb — [Name]" (`D1-QA` 00:31:14); "Top 5 suburbs near Las Vegas" (`D1-VIP` 00:18:03).
- **Price-point rule:** shoot content **one notch above** your average sale — $600–800k market → tour $800k–$1M homes. People love to dream (`D1-GS` 00:50:52).
- **One avatar per channel.** Don't blend relocation + investors (emotional vs. logical buyers) — *"half of your videos are going to go in the toilet on one side and half on the other"* (`D1-VIP` 00:09:28).
- Channel name: **"Living in [Driver City] with [Your Name]"**. Duplicate names don't matter — *"there could be 5 million Living in Boise with Jesse Dau"*; nobody owns a channel name (`D1-QA` 00:24:41, `D2-QA` 00:19:10). The **@handle never took off** — people search your name, not your handle (`D1-QA` 00:32:15).

## Titles

**Home tour formula** (the single most repeated tactic of the week):

> **"Inside / Tour this [emotional adjective] [new] home in / near [Driver City, State]"**

- "Tour this **affordable** new home near Houston, Texas" · "Tour this **luxury** home in Houston, Texas" (`D1-QA` 00:15:53)
- "Tour this **incredibly affordable** house in College Station, Texas" · "Tour this **insanely beautiful** home in College Station" (`D2-QA` 00:28:38)
- Pick the adjective to match **your** avatar — don't copy Jesse's verbatim (`D1-QA` 00:53:55).

**Never put in a title or thumbnail:**
- **A dollar amount.** "What $500,000 gets you in Boise" alienates every other price band — a Bay Area viewer reads $500k as a *"dumpster box"* (`D1-QA` 00:15:07, `D1-VIP` 00:46:20). Say "most affordable" instead.
- **"For sale."** *"'New home for sale' is the most searched keyword on Google for local traffic, so you're just gonna blow up your local traffic"* (`D1-QA` 00:16:33, `D1-GS` 00:53:01).
- **News / hype titles.** "Everything new and coming to [City] in 2026", "Why billion-dollar investors are investing in [City]", "Billion dollar megaplex coming to [City]", "Where old money lives in [City]", "Why Elon Musk moved Tesla to [City]", "Why [company] is building an AI data center in [City]". These pull **95% local** traffic and turn you into a newscaster (`D1-QA` 00:38:04, `D1-VIP` 00:34:55, `D2-GS` 00:21:32).
  - Cautionary tale: **Ian in Austin** ran a channel doing **$30M/yr**, switched to megaplex/Elon-Musk local news content, and **closings went to zero**. He started a fresh channel and was back in leads in **under 30 days** (`D1-VIP` Part 2 00:03:50).

**Also:** delete or retitle any video with big impressions and a nonsense title, and any video whose audience is overwhelmingly local (`D1-QA` 00:37:07).

## Thumbnails

- **Run 3 thumbnails per video** as an A/B/C test. Every time a viewer logs back in, YouTube rotates them — another bite at the apple. *"It's like having three different products in the store"* (`D1-VIP` 00:42:54).
- **Only default to a "winner" at 20%+ variation** — YouTube's own guidance, because YouTube measures **watch time**. TubeBuddy defaults to 3% because it measures **CTR**. If variation is small, keep all three running (`D1-VIP` 00:43:23).
- Native split test: upload screen → thumbnail section → ⋯ → **"Set up a split test"**, up to 3 thumbnails, optionally with different titles (`D1-QA` 00:51:47).
- **AI thumbnails are allowed** — YouTube won't penalise the channel. The only risk is that they *look* AI and never get clicked: *"you got to take all of the fake AI out of the thumbnail"* (`D2-QA` 00:23:45).
- Packaging is secondary to the video: *"You can have subpar thumbnails, subpar titles, but a great video and the video will still do good. But if you have a bad video and great thumbnails and a great title, the video ain't going to do jack"* (`D2-GS` 00:45:54, `D1-QA` 00:39:19).

## Scripting, hooks and retention

**The home-tour retention structure** (`D2-GS` 00:14:17–00:16:43) — the highest-leverage
tactic in the whole challenge:

1. **Never open with the price.** Only ~**3%** of the audience is actively buying right now; leading with a number narrows you to a subset of a subset.
2. **Hook + call-out in under 5 seconds:**
   > *"So you're thinking about moving out here to Boise, Idaho. Well, in this video I'm going to be showing you guys one of the most affordable houses that I found in the entire area. So let's get into it."*
3. **Put the CTA immediately BEFORE the price reveal at the end:**
   > *"All right, so I know you guys all want to know the price of this house. Well, before I tell you that — remember I'm the top agent here in the market, helping people like you relocate every single day. So if you're thinking about moving out here, give me a call, shoot me a text, send me an email. The price of this house is $400,000. And if you want to see more like it, go ahead and click on this link."*
4. This one change turns the "quarter-pipe" retention drop-off into a **flat line to the end of the video**.

**Every video opens by naming the viewer:** *"So you're thinking about moving to [City, State]"* — makes the viewer look in the mirror (`D1-GS` 00:42:23).

**The "undercover call to action"** — weave your lender/partner into the narrative instead of interviewing them. Don't do podcast-style interviews. Give the lender a role, don't let them get into lending weeds, then close soft: *"give us a call, shoot us a text, send us an email — no strings attached, we're not going to force you into any contracts, you don't have to pay us any money"* (`D1-VIP` 00:58:23).

> *"On the YouTube channel we're giving them what they want, but then we're giving them
> the messages of what they need to hear."* — `D1-VIP` 01:00:32

**Slow down and go granular.** Don't say "stainless steel appliances" — talk about the
fridge, oven, stove, dishwasher and sink individually; that's 90 seconds of content you
just skipped. Pretend you're your best friend's eyes and ears. Build **signature details**
you always comment on (Jesse: single-bowl offset-drain sinks; his wife: big drawers) so
returning viewers get an inside joke (`D1-QA` 00:47:38).

**Never interview the builder.** *You* are the character in the show. You can absolutely
narrate construction quality yourself — conditioned vs. unconditioned crawl spaces, 12"
vs. 18" framing offsets, vapor barrier, roof underlayment (`D1-QA` 00:25:25).

### AI and scripts
- **The "AI 20" rule:** AI may do **80%**, human intelligence must do the other **20%** — polishing, proofing, making it sound like you (`D1-QA` 00:04:07).
- YouTube has **built-in AI language and imagery detection**; index too high and it throttles the video (`D1-QA` 00:03:34, `D1-GS` 00:13:44).
- Tells that get you smelled out: "five things no other agent is telling you", em-dashes, "this city's a vibe" (`D1-QA` 00:05:11).
- > *"AI takes about three paragraphs to communicate what I can say in one sentence. So I have a massive advantage."* — `D1-QA` 00:06:00
- > *"If you're reading an AI script, my AI avatar is actually better than you — it's more consistent and it can create more volume."* — `D2-QA` 00:12:19
- **The moat:** an AI avatar can replicate a talking head. It cannot replicate a vlog or a walk-through home tour. *"AI can't disrupt truth. AI can't disrupt demonstration of content."* (`D1-QA` 00:36:57, `D2-QA` 00:11:44).
- Framing for the whole era: *"We are moving away from an attention-based economy into a trust-based economy."* (`D1-VIP` 00:56:36).

## Filming, gear and format

- **Shoot 1080p / 60fps. Do not shoot 4K.** Storage burden, slow transfers to offshore editors, and no benefit — *"the only reason you would ever need 4K is if you were going to start playing these videos at the movie theater"* (`D2-GS` 00:19:43).
- **Current kit:** DJI **Osmo Pocket 3** (Creator Combo) + **DJI wireless mic**. Optional drone. That's it — the old GoPro-7-plus-boom-mic rig is *"way too cumbersome"* (`D1-QA` 00:20:32).
- **Green screens are played out.** Shoot talking heads on location — outdoors, in new construction, in front of a house — not against a blank white wall (`D1-QA` 00:34:40).
- If you could only ever make one format: **vlogs**, because a vlog can absorb home tours, communities and talking-head information. *"King of the vlog is king of the city"* (`D1-QA` 00:36:41).
- Production value is not the constraint: *"I literally have a whiteboard and a freaking painted wall behind me and I've been doing this for 10 years — if you deliver good information it doesn't matter what it looks like."* Audio quality does matter (`D1-VIP` 00:57:28).
- **Bulk film.** Jesse's wife shoots **48 videos in one session** and does not change clothes between them — 70% of viewers have never seen you before (`D2-QA` 00:03:05).
- Time budget: talking-head ~1 hour to make; vlog tours 2–4 hours (`D2-GS` 00:08:26).
- Storage: a **2TB** external drive with four folders — raw footage / episodes / generic B-roll / photos — is plenty (`D2-QA` 00:20:25).
- Getting into homes: call the listing agent, ask about the property, let them talk, then *"when I'm in your property, would you mind if I shot some content?"* Afterwards send them the impressions and watch-hours and put their name in the description. ~90% of homes they film go pending the week the video drops (`D1-QA` 00:25:25–00:30:39).

## Lead conversion and the business behind it

- **One relocation buyer = 4 transactions** (their outgoing sale referral, their purchase, their faster resale, their referrals) (`D2-GS` 00:24:37).
- **Referral income is the quiet engine.** Jesse pulled three uncashed referral checks out of his mailbox that week — **$9,000, $11,000 and ~$11,500** (`D2-GS` 00:25:29). Referrals net better than local deals: no transaction-coordination fee, no drive time.
- **Assumptive-close referral script** — when a relocation client still has a home to sell, assume they're using your partner in that market, then sell the partner on service (communication, 2-week post-closing occupancy, cleaners, handyman touch-ups). Result: they sign a 3.5–4% listing agreement there while you hold a **5% BBA** here (`D2-QA` 00:07:08).
- Barrett (Austin): **$100k GCI months, capped at 8 Zoom calls a week** — the Zoom call, not lead volume, is the throttle (`D2-GS` 01:00:41).
- YouTube leads don't negotiate: *"I tell them it's 3% plus a $350 transaction fee and they're all in. There's no convincing."* (`D1-GS` 00:55:38).
- **Retargeting stack:** YouTube channel → website with a relocation-guide lead magnet → Meta pixel → retarget visitors at **$10/day** (`D2-QA` 00:26:01, `D1-VIP` 00:28:07).
- Attribute each inbound lead to whatever video is live that day, so you can see which content actually pays (`D1-GS` 00:58:31).
- The buyer demographic skews old: YouTube's average buying age is **over 50**, and Jesse cites ~**80%** of buyers as 50+ (`D1-VIP` 00:54:14).

## Notable Q&A answers

- **Multiple channels?** No second channel until you've maxed the first — a fully-worked single channel tops out near **$1.5M GCI** (Level 8 of their L1–L8 value ladder) (`D1-QA` 00:11:15).
- **When to actually restart a channel:** if it's diluted — returning viewers near zero, ~100% new audience, ~90% local traffic — start fresh. You can reuse relocation videos, **but not on both channels; YouTube will strike you for duplicates** (`D1-VIP` Part 2 00:02:54–00:03:25).
- **Tags/hashtags?** Use them, but they're worth roughly **1–2 views** each. Never use a competitor's name (`D1-QA` 00:38:58, `D2-QA` 00:04:28).
- **Private/unlisted videos?** No algorithmic effect — **unless an unlisted video sits in a playlist**. Remove them from playlists (`D2-QA` 00:05:15).
- **Is 30 minutes too long?** *"There's no such thing as a too long video. There is such thing as a too long, unengaging video."* But not on a brand-new channel — no trust built yet (`D2-QA` 00:05:37).
- **TubeBuddy vs vidIQ?** TubeBuddy, with caveats (use it for driver-city search volume and to compare your last-5-video average against the weakest video in a term's top 20). vidIQ leans on vanity metrics: *"I've never seen somebody absolutely destroying it on YouTube that uses vidIQ at a high level"* (`D1-QA` 00:42:46).
- **YouTube Live?** Generally no — lives pull local traffic and die faster than home tours; many agents have killed channels with them. Run the event on Zoom registration instead (`D1-QA` 00:59:34).
- **Same channel name as a competitor?** Fine — add your own name. But make clear you're *not* affiliated with them; Jesse has seen clients sign a BBA with the wrong "Living in" agent thinking they were the same team (`D2-QA` 00:19:10).
- **Where to find editors?** Fiverr, online job boards, local Facebook groups (`D2-QA` 00:24:27).
- **Tom Ferry's "Google Trifecta"?** Google→YouTube data flow is real but *"external traffic is very minimal."* Jesse's preferred trifecta is YouTube + website + Meta retargeting (`D2-QA` 00:24:43).
- **Are LLMs a traffic source now?** Increasingly — Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity and Claude all cite YouTube videos, and *"everything is signal data"* (`D2-QA` 00:25:25).
